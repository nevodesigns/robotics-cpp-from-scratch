id: E-RT-0009
title: The highest priority task waited for the medium one, by way of the low one
match: (priority inversion|high priority task was blocked|waiting for a lock held by a lower priority task)
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 07-15-priority-is-a-share

## Symptom

A task of the highest priority misses its deadline although the CPU has spare time and nothing of higher priority is running. The
lateness appears only when a medium priority task is also busy, and not on the bench where it is not. A watchdog resets the system
and the log says the control task did not run.

## Cause

Priority inversion. A low priority task holds a lock. The high priority task wakes and needs it, so it waits. A medium priority task
wakes. It does not need the lock, it outranks the low task, so it runs, and it keeps running while it has work. The low task cannot
finish the work that would release the lock for as long as the medium one runs. The high task is waiting for the medium one, which it
outranks, by way of a task it was never supposed to wait for.

Simulated, a tick at a time, who ran each tick:

| | trace | High finished | after it woke |
|---|---|---|---|
| without inheritance | `LLHMMMMMMMMMMLLLHL` | tick 16 | **15 ticks** |
| with inheritance | `LLHLLLHMMMMMMMMMML` | tick 6 | **5 ticks** |

Medium finished at tick 12 without inheritance, **before High**. A task that outranks nothing in the picture finishing first is
the signature.

This is the bug that repeatedly reset the Mars Pathfinder lander in 1997. The fix that was uploaded to it was turning priority
inheritance on.

## Fix

Priority **inheritance**: a task holding a lock runs at the highest priority of any task waiting for it.

```cpp
best = std::max(best, effective_priority(tasks, holder, other, inheritance, depth + 1));
```

Low, holding the lock High needs, runs as if it were High, finishes, releases, and High goes. Medium pays: it finished at tick 16
instead of 12, four ticks later. That is the cost, and it is the point.

On a real system, use a mutex with the protocol: `pthread_mutexattr_setprotocol(PTHREAD_PRIO_INHERIT)`. `std::mutex` does not
promise it. Or do not share a lock between priorities, which is what lesson 07-12's seqlock and lesson 07-02's queue are for.
