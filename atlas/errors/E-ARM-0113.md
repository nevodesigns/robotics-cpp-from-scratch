## Symptom

A gantry built larger to the same design is much less accurate than the small one. The large machine is built and its accuracy is a fraction of the small one's.

## Cause

The beam grew in length and not in section. A crossbeam supported at its ends sags F L cubed over 48 E I: a 1.0 m beam under 50 N sagged 50 micrometres (a stiffness of 1.0 MN/m), and twice the span gave 8 times the sag.

A beam's sag goes as the cube of its span over its stiffness. Doubling the span with the same section makes the sag eight times as large, and the stiffness eight times as small, which also lowers the first resonance.

## Fix

Grow the beam's section with its length, or support it, and work the sag out before building. Check the resonance too: that stiffness with 5 kg moving is 71 Hz, and a heavier head lowers it.

```cpp
const double sag = rc::arm::beam_sag(50.0, span, ei);   // 50 um at 1.0 m, 400 um at 2.0 m
```

A beam that grows in depth gains stiffness as the cube of its depth, which is why big gantries have deep beams.
