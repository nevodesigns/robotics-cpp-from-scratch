id: E-CPP-0036
title: Any and all confused, so one fault reads as every fault
match: any is not all, and the difference is a whole mask
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 01-10-the-fault-that-never-fired
---

## Symptom

A check against a group of flags answers wrongly, but only when more than one
of them is involved. A driver reports that every fault is live when one is, or
reports a composite condition clear when half of it is set.

Single flags behave perfectly, which is what makes it slow to find.

## Cause

Two different questions share one shape.

```cpp
(value & mask) != 0      // is ANY bit of mask set
(value & mask) == mask   // are ALL bits of mask set
```

With a single bit mask they are the same answer, always. The moment the mask
covers two bits they diverge, and whichever one you wrote is the one you get.

Measured with only the over temperature bit set, against a mask of all four
known faults:

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| `has_any(status, kAllFaults)` | true |
| `has_all(status, kAllFaults)` | false |

Both are correct answers to their own question. The bug is asking the other
one.

There is a related trap at the empty mask. `has_all(value, 0)` is true, because
every one of no bits is set, and `has_any(value, 0)` is false. Those fall out of
the natural expressions without special cases, and a hand written version that
special cases zero usually gets one of them backwards.

## Fix

**Name the question.** `rc::core::has_any` and `rc::core::has_all`, so the call
site says which one it is and a reviewer can see it without reconstructing the
comparison.

**Test with a mask of more than one bit.** A suite written against single flags
cannot tell the two apart, which is why this reaches a release. One test with a
two bit mask and one bit set separates them.

**Say which you mean when a flag group appears in a requirement.** "Report a
fault if any of these are set" and "the arm is ready when all of these are set"
are different sentences that often arrive written as the same one.

**Check the flag constants are single bits.** `rc::core::is_single_bit` over
every constant at startup catches a mask transcribed as `0x03` where `0x02` was
meant, which turns every single flag test in the system into a two bit test and
makes this bug appear where no group was intended at all.
