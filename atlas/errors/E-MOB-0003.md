## Symptom

Grasps succeed close to the base and miss further out, and the misses are sideways, across the line from the base to the hand. A base that has just turned on the spot is the usual case: its heading has the largest error, and the next grasp misses sideways.

## Cause

The base's heading is a degree out. A position error of the base is carried to the hand one for one (5 mm gave 5 mm), but a heading error is multiplied by the distance from the base's centre to the hand: one degree at 0.9 m is 15.7 mm.

Localisation gives the base's heading with an error of about a degree, and the arm reaches out from the base. A heading error turns the whole arm round the base's centre, so its effect is the angle times the lever. Short reaches hide it; the arm at full stretch shows it.

## Fix

Budget the heading error as a lever, and measure at the hand (a camera on the arm) when the lever is long.

```cpp
const double e = rc::mobile::hand_error_from_base(base, hand, 0.005, 0.01745);   // 15.7 mm of it from the heading at 0.9 m
```

The cheapest cure is not a better localiser but a measurement from the hand (33-07): it removes the base's errors altogether.
