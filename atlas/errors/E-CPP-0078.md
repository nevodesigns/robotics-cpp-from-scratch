title: A name made by a scheme where a small change makes a small difference
match: changing one character changes about half of the 64 bits, wherever it is
match: expected this to be true: mean > 28.0
platforms: linux, windows

## Symptom

Two texts that differ in one character get names that differ in one place, or two different files get the same name. A tool that trusts equal names to mean equal content
then believes two different files are one.

## Cause

A sum of the bytes, or the length, is a name, and a weak one. Changing a character moves it by a little, and swapping two characters does not move it at all.

A good scheme does the opposite: every input bit changes about half of the output bits. Measured with FNV-1a, one character changed in a 200 character text in every place in turn: a mean of
**32.4 of 64** bits differed, between 16 and 44, wherever the change was. A thousand different texts got a thousand different names.

## Fix

Mix every byte in with an xor and a multiplication by a large odd number, the way FNV-1a does:

```cpp
hash ^= static_cast<Id>(static_cast<unsigned char>(text[i]));
hash *= 1099511628211ULL;
```
