## Symptom

Two sensors that agree on a heading, a little either side of straight back, are averaged and
the result points straight ahead. A fused heading that is exactly wrong, by half a turn, when
both inputs are right. It only happens near the back of the circle.

## Cause

An arithmetic mean of 3.1 and -3.1 is zero. Zero points the opposite way from both of them.
The mean treats an angle as a quantity on a line, and an angle is a direction on a circle.

Angles are averaged as the directions they are: add the unit vectors, `(cos a, sin a)` each
times its weight, and take the direction of the sum. For 3.1 and -3.1 that is 3.142, straight
back, which is the answer.

There is one more case a real averager has to admit. If the vectors cancel, as they do for 0
and pi, there is no mean direction at all: the resultant has length zero and `atan2` of zero and
zero is not an answer to anything. `circular_mean` reports that as undefined instead of
returning a zero that looks like a heading.

## Fix

```cpp
sx += w * std::cos(angle);
sy += w * std::sin(angle);
defined = std::hypot(sx, sy) > 1e-12;
return defined ? std::atan2(sy, sx) : 0.0;
```

and check `defined`. A fused heading that cannot say which way it points is information a
controller needs, since the right response to it is to stop turning on a number that is made up.
