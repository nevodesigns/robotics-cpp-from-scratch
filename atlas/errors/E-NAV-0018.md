id: E-NAV-0018
title: Clearance measured on the path rather than on the robot
match: clearance of the path is not clearance of the robot
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 16-07-the-rover-that-fits
---

## Symptom

Everything checks out and the robot still touches things. The map is inflated
for the robot's radius, the planned route clears by more than that radius, the
follower reports a few centimetres of cross track error, and there are scrape
marks down one side of the machine.

Every number in the report is correct.

## Cause

Three different things spend the same margin and only one of them was counted.

Driving a route planned with a 0.25 m inflation, whose own tightest point
clears 0.316 m, with the follower from lesson 16-04 and a pose 100 ms old:

| lookahead | robot's own worst clearance |
|---|---|
| the path itself | 0.316 m |
| 0.30 m | 0.337 m |
| 0.60 m | 0.255 m |
| 1.00 m | **0.068 m** |

At a metre of lookahead the robot passes 6.8 cm from an obstacle, on a route
planned with a 25 cm radius inflated into the map. Nothing failed. The follower
cut the corners of the route, which is what a lookahead does and what
`E-NAV-0009` measures, and the corner cut came out of the same margin the
inflation was supposed to provide.

The three claims on that margin are:

- **the robot's width**, which the inflation covers;
- **the follower's corner cut**, about 0.27 of the lookahead for a right angle;
- **the estimator's error**, which is speed times its latency plus its noise.

Add the map's own quantisation and you have a fourth, worth up to half a cell.

## Fix

Measure the thing the requirement is about, which is the robot, not the path.

```cpp
const double here = rc::nav::clearance_at(map, pose.x, pose.y);
```

Log the worst value over a drive and compare that against the requirement. The
planner's clearance and the follower's cross track error are both useful and
neither is this number.

Then budget the margin explicitly rather than hoping the inflation covers
everything:

```
inflation = robot radius + 0.27 * lookahead + speed * estimator latency + half a cell
```

Every term there is measurable and three of them were measured in earlier
lessons. The alternative is discovering the sum on a rack.

The relationship also runs the other way, which is the useful part: shortening
the lookahead buys clearance directly, and so does reducing the estimator's
latency. A robot that cannot fit down an aisle at full speed may fit down it
slowly, and now you can say by how much rather than guessing.
