title: A slow, careful rise
match: the careful slow stand-up overheated the knees
match: standing up gently was worse for the motors
match: a 12 kg robot can rise, a slow rise uses more of the knee's thermal life than a fast one, and the heaviest bodies are limited by torque and by heat
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 36-03-getting-up
---

## Symptom

A rise done slowly, to be gentle, leaves the knees hotter than a quick one and sometimes trips the thermal limit. The robot that "gets up carefully" is the one that overheats.

## Cause

Heat is torque squared over time. In this count the torque at each height is the same however fast the rise is, so a slow rise holds the same torques for longer. The rise in 1 s used 0.034 of the knee's thermal life and the rise in 6 s used 0.202: six times as much. Turned around, the heaviest body that could rise within the heat was 40.6 kg in 1 s and only 24.8 kg in 6 s.

## Fix

Rise promptly, within the torque, and check the heat for the time you plan to take.

```cpp
const rc::legged::RisePlan quick = rc::legged::rise_plan(leg, m, knee, 0.12, 0.28, 1.0, 50);   // life_used 0.034
const rc::legged::RisePlan slow = rc::legged::rise_plan(leg, m, knee, 0.12, 0.28, 6.0, 50);    // life_used 0.202
const double secs = quick.torque_ok && quick.heat_ok ? 1.0 : 6.0;
```

The count leaves out the torque that accelerating the body needs, which a very quick rise adds. That is the limit on the other side.
