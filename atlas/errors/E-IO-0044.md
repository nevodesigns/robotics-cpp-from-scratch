## Symptom

The loss counter says the link is nearly perfect and the robot has plainly missed
seconds of data. The counter was read at the end of a run with a cable that came out
once, and it reports a fraction of what went missing.

## Cause

A sequence number counts a gap between two messages it saw. When the connection is
made again the tracker is started over, because the device's counter may have
restarted and a tracker kept across that counts phantom losses, which lesson 08-13
measured. A restarted tracker has nothing to compare the first message with, so
whatever was lost while the cable was out is in no counter at all.

Measured, sixty seconds with the cable out for three seconds at twenty:

| | frames |
|---|---|
| sent by the device | 6000 |
| delivered | 5609 |
| missing | 391 |
| counted lost by the sequence numbers | 90 |
| in no counter | 301 |

The 301 are the three seconds of the outage at a hundred frames a second, plus the
frame at the boundary. The 90 are real losses on the live connection: dropped and
corrupted bytes.

## Fix

Say which is which. The loss counter is **loss while connected**, and the outage is a
separate figure: how many times the connection was made again, and for how long it was
down. `Health::reconnects` carries the first. A caller that wants the second keeps the
time it noticed the disconnect and the time it came back.

Report both, and do not present the first as the whole story. A link that says "0.09
percent loss" and has been down for five percent of the run has told the truth about
one half of it.
