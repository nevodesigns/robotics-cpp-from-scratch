title: A loop that lost cycles and said nothing, or waited an hour for a clock that moved

## Symptom

A robot is slower than specified and no test fails. Or a loop stops entirely after the
system clock was adjusted, and resumes an hour later, or never.

## Cause

Two things the loop does not tell anyone.

**Dropped cycles that were never counted.** A loop that skips a slot when it overruns
is doing the right thing, and doing it invisibly. Measured: work of 8, 14, 35, 2, 2 ms
in a 10 ms period started its cycles at 0, 10, 30, 70, 80, and **4 slots were dropped**:
one for the 14 ms cycle and three for the 35. With no count, that is a loop running at
about two thirds of its rate, reported as fine.

It also costs more than people expect. Late by **one nanosecond** drops the whole slot:
the cycle that finishes at 40 ms and 1 ns waits until 50. That is the price of never
bursting, and it is better stated than discovered.

**A deadline from a clock that moved.** A loop whose next start is a second ahead of a
clock that was stepped back sleeps for a second. Waiting for a deadline that has become
distant stops the loop.

## Fix

Count what was dropped and expose it:

```cpp
long long missed() const { return missed_; }
```

and log it, or raise it, when it is not zero. A control loop with a nonzero count has
been running slower than it was asked to and someone should know.

Guard the wait:

```cpp
if (wait > period_) {            // more than a whole period: the clock moved, or called early
  deadline_ = now + period_;
  return period_;
}
```

Measured: a pacer whose grid said a full second away, called with the clock at zero,
returned one period. A period of zero or less becomes one nanosecond, so the first late
cycle cannot divide by zero.

Use `std::chrono::steady_clock` for the loop's own timing. It never goes backwards, which
is the reason the guard is a second line of defence and not the first.
