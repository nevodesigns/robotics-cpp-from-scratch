## Symptom

An angle between two directions is reported as exactly zero when the directions
plainly differ, or comes back as NaN and then spreads through every later
calculation. It happens for nearly parallel vectors, which in a path follower is
almost all the time.

## Cause

The textbook formula, acos of the dot product over the product of the lengths,
has three faults.

Cosine is flat near zero, so a tiny error in the dot product becomes a large
error in the angle. Measured on a real machine, two directions genuinely 1e-8
radians apart give an acos of exactly 0.0. The angle is not merely inaccurate, it
is gone.

Rounding can make that ratio slightly greater than one, and acos of anything
above one is NaN.

And acos answers zero to pi, so it has no sign and cannot tell left from right.

## Fix

Use both products:

```cpp
const double angle = std::atan2(cross(a, b), dot(a, b));
```

atan2 is well conditioned everywhere, has no domain to leave, and returns a
signed angle already in the range minus pi to pi.

Where acos genuinely is the right tool, clamp its argument before the call:

```cpp
const double ratio = std::min(1.0, std::max(-1.0, value));
```
