## Symptom

A simulated die, a random choice or a random delay turns up some values more often than others. The difference is small for small ranges and nobody notices, until a range is large
and a third of the choices are quietly favoured.

## Cause

`next() % n` is fair only when n divides the range of next() exactly. Otherwise the draws wrap round, and the smaller remainders arise one more time than the rest.

Measured with n of 3,000,000,000: the share of draws below 1,294,967,296 was **0.604** with a remainder, against **0.433** from `below(n)`, and 0.432 is what a fair draw gives.

## Fix

Throw away the few draws that would cause the imbalance, and take the remainder of the rest:

```cpp
const unsigned int threshold = (0u - n) % n;
unsigned int r = next();
while (r < threshold) r = next();
return r % n;
```
