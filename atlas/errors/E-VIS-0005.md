## Symptom

An object count that is sometimes right. A marker that splits into two when it
is slightly out of focus or slightly further away. Two markers that merge into
one when they happen to line up at a corner.

Or: the count is right and the robot reaches for a point that is not on the
object at all.

## Cause

Two decisions that look like facts.

**What counts as one thing.** Two pixels touching only at a corner are the same
region or not, and nothing about the picture says which. A diagonal chain of
five pixels:

```
  .......
  .#.....
  ..#....
  ...#...
  ....#..
  .....#.
  .......
```

| connectivity | blobs |
|---|---|
| four | **5** |
| eight | **1** |

A crack in a surface wants one answer and two markers that happen to touch at a
corner want the other. A solid shape is one blob under both, which is why a test
on a square cannot tell them apart.

**Where the thing is.** The centre of mass and the centre of the bounding box
are different points for anything that is not symmetric. An L six pixels tall:

| | position |
|---|---|
| centre of mass | 2.36, 4.64 |
| centre of the box | 3.50, 3.50 |
| apart | **1.61 pixels** |

A quarter of the object. On a rectangle they agree exactly, which is again what
a test on a square marker shows.

## Fix

Choose both, deliberately, and write down why.

```cpp
const auto blobs = rc::vision::find_blobs(image, threshold,
                                          rc::vision::Connectivity::eight);
```

**Eight connectivity** joins things that touch at a corner, which is usually
right for a physical object photographed at an angle, where a thin part of it
may be one pixel wide.

**Four connectivity** keeps them apart, which is right when the count matters
more than the completeness: separate markers, holes, defects.

For the position: **the centroid is where the thing is**, and it is what a
gripper or a heading should aim at. **The box centre is where the space it
occupies is**, and it is what a collision check or a crop wants. They are
different questions and the code should say which is being asked.

Two more habits.

**Test with an asymmetric shape and a diagonal.** A square marker passes every
version of both of these bugs.

**Use the pixel count.** It is free, it is already there, and a blob far larger
or smaller than the object can be is the cheapest rejection test there is.
