title: A one pixel line that is two pixels on a better screen
match: a pen of width one is not one pixel wide
platforms: linux, windows

## Symptom

Gridlines, axes and borders look crisp on one screen and heavy or blurred on
another. A chart that was carefully balanced becomes a mess of thick grey lines
on a high resolution display, and the numbers are harder to read rather than
easier.

## Cause

`setWidth(1)` does not mean one pixel. It means one **logical** unit, which is
as many device pixels as the ratio says.

Measured, a horizontal line across a surface at a ratio of 2:

| pen | ink | device rows |
|---|---|---|
| `setWidth(0)` | 200 | **1** |
| `setWidth(1)` | 400 | **2** |
| `setWidth(2)` | 800 | 4 |

So the grid somebody tuned to be unobtrusive at a ratio of 1 is twice as heavy
at 2 and three times at 3, while the text beside it stays the same weight
because a font is laid out in logical units and rendered at the device's
resolution.

Width zero is the part people do not expect. In Qt a pen of width zero is
**cosmetic**: it means the thinnest line the device can draw, not no line at
all, and it stays one device pixel whatever the ratio.

## Fix

Decide, for each line, which of the two it should be, and say so.

**One device pixel, always.** Rules, gridlines, hairline borders: things whose
job is to be visible and not to be noticed.

```cpp
inline QPen hairline(const QColor& colour) {
  QPen pen(colour);
  pen.setWidth(0);
  return pen;
}
```

**A real thickness in logical units.** A trace on a chart, an outline that is
part of the design, anything that should look the same size on every screen.
`setWidth(2)` for two logical units, and it will be four device pixels at a
ratio of 2, which is what "the same size" means.

The mistake is not choosing either, and `setWidth(1)` is what you get by not
choosing: too thin to be a deliberate thickness and too thick to be a hairline.

Two things worth knowing alongside.

**A cosmetic pen ignores the painter's transform as well**, so a hairline stays
one pixel under a scale, which is usually what a grid wants and never what a
drawn object wants.

**Test the row count, not the appearance.** Rendering a line and counting the
device rows it inked is three lines of test and it pins the decision, on every
screen ratio you care about.
