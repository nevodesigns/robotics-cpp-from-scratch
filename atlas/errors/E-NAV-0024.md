## Symptom

A calibration routine that drives one test, a straight line, or a spin, and reports two wheel scales and a wheelbase.
The numbers fit that test perfectly and are wrong for every other move. A calibration done at a different speed, on a
different floor, gives a different answer, and none of them make the square close.

## Cause

There are three unknowns, the left wheel's scale, the right's, and the wheelbase, and one move does not tell them
apart. A straight drive gives two things: how far it went, which is the average of the two scales, and how much it
veered, which is their difference over the wheelbase. A spin gives the third: the rotation depends on how far apart the
wheels are, and is the only move that does.

The three are found in order, because each needs the one before.

```text
mean scale   = arc / nominal distance                           (the straight drive)
wheelbase    = 2 * mean * spin travel / (rotation * base)       (the spin, and the mean)
difference   = veer * real wheelbase / nominal distance         (the straight drive again)
left, right  = mean -/+ difference / 2
```

What is measured on a straight drive is the chord of an arc. The two differ by the sinc of half the veer, which is
taken out.

With exact measurements on five different robots, from a left wheel 1.2 % big and a wheelbase 2 % wide to both wheels
5 % big, the three came back to within a part in a hundred thousand. The simulated robot is moved in a thousand pieces
and is exact to about that.

## Fix

Two moves, a drive and a spin, and the algebra above, in that order. A move of nothing says nothing, so if the base,
either nominal distance, or the measured rotation is zero, the answer is the defaults with `valid` false and nothing is
divided by it.
