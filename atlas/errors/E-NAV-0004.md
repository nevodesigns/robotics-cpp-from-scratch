## Symptom

A filter that works, and not as well as it should. The estimate is better than
either input on its own, so nothing looks broken, and it is quietly giving away
most of what fusion was worth.

Two shapes, depending on the direction of the mistake. An estimate that lags
behind reality and responds sluggishly to measurements it should believe. Or one
that jitters, chasing every noisy reading as though it were news.

## Cause

The variances handed to the filter are not the variances the sensors actually
have.

Measured over two thousand steps, on the same data, changing only what the
filter was told:

| what the filter believes | error |
|---|---|
| the truth | 0.0237 m |
| its odometry is ten times better than it is | 0.0484 m |
| its odometry is ten times worse than it is | 0.0530 m |
| its sensor is ten times better than it is | 0.0530 m |

Being wrong in either direction roughly doubles the error, against dead
reckoning alone at 0.058 and the sensor alone at 0.098. So the filter is
forgiving, and most of its value is gone.

Read the last two rows together, because they are the useful part. They are the
**same number**, and that is not a coincidence. The gain depends only on the
**ratio** of the two variances:

```text
gain = prior / (prior + measurement)
```

Scale both by ten and nothing changes. So "my odometry is worse than I thought"
and "my sensor is better than I thought" are the same statement, and there is
only one quantity to get right rather than two.

## Fix

Measure the variances rather than guessing them, which is easier than it sounds
for both kinds of input.

**A sensor**: hold the robot still, record a few hundred readings, and take
their variance. That is the number, and it takes a minute.

**Odometry**: drive a known distance repeatedly and take the variance of the
error, per metre. Lesson 16-01 is about which path to drive, and it matters:
some of these errors are invisible on a straight line.

Then two habits.

**Express uncertainty as variance, and say so in the name.** A sensor described
as accurate to five centimetres has a standard deviation of 0.05 and a variance
of 0.0025, and passing one where the other belongs is wrong by a factor of
twenty. `measurement_variance` is a better parameter name than `noise`.

**Tune the ratio, not both numbers.** Since only the ratio matters, fix the
sensor variance at its measured value and adjust the motion variance alone.
Turning two knobs that do the same thing is how filter tuning becomes folklore.
