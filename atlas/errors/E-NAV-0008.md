title: A lookahead shorter than the error in the pose it steers by

match: the smallest workable lookahead is speed times latency

## Symptom

A path follower that was steady in simulation weaves on the robot, or leaves the
path entirely and drives away in a straight line. Shortening the lookahead to
tighten it up makes it worse.

The same follower is fine at walking pace and unusable when somebody turns the
speed up.

## Cause

The follower does not steer by where the robot is. It steers by where the robot
believes it is, and the lookahead decides how hard it acts on that belief.

Aiming at a point a lookahead away, an error of `e` in the pose turns into a
steering command of roughly `2e / L²`. Halving the lookahead quadruples what the
same error does to the steering. When the error approaches the lookahead, the
target point is inside the error and the command is noise.

Measured on a right angle corner at 1 m/s, with the pose 100 ms behind:

| lookahead | rms | steering churn |
|---|---|---|
| 0.10 m | **lost** | 0.0895 |
| 0.20 m | 0.0104 | 0.0941 |
| 0.40 m | 0.0077 | 0.0082 |
| 0.80 m | 0.0264 | 0.0034 |

"Lost" is not bad tracking. The robot left the path and did not come back.

And the threshold is exactly the distance the robot covers while the estimate
catches up:

| speed | speed x 0.1 s | 0.05 | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 m/s | 0.05 | lost | 0.0023 | 0.0028 | 0.0098 |
| 1.0 m/s | 0.10 | lost | lost | 0.0104 | 0.0077 |
| 2.0 m/s | 0.20 | lost | lost | lost | 0.0415 |
| 4.0 m/s | 0.40 | lost | lost | lost | lost |

Every lookahead at or below `speed x latency` fails, and the first one above it
works. At every speed.

Noise behaves differently and is worth telling apart. With 2 cm of pose noise
and no latency, a lookahead of 0.10 m tracked the path *better* than 0.80 m did,
and moved the steering a hundred times as much doing it. Noise is paid for in
the actuator; latency is paid for in the path.

## Fix

Scale the lookahead with speed, and floor it above the estimate's own error.

```cpp
const double lookahead = std::max(min_lookahead, gain * speed);
```

`gain` is the estimate's latency with margin, so a pose 100 ms behind wants a
gain of at least 0.2 s. `min_lookahead` covers standing starts and has to clear
the noise rather than the latency.

Then reduce the latency, because it is buying nothing. Every millisecond removed
from the estimate is lookahead you get to spend on cornering instead, and lesson
15-03 has the measurements for where that latency lives.

A fixed lookahead is a maximum speed nobody wrote down. If yours is a constant,
write the speed it was chosen at in the comment beside it, so the next person
speeding the robot up has something to find.

The corner cutting that a long lookahead buys is `E-NAV-0009`.
