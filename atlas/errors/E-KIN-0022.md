## Symptom

A move that looked harmless in the planner trips a joint speed limit, or a following error, near the end of
travel, and only when the tool is moving outward along the arm. The tool speed was modest. The joint that fails is
the elbow, and it fails at the point where the arm is nearly straight.

## Cause

Moving the tool in a straight line at an even speed demands whatever joint speeds that takes, and near full extension
the Jacobian is nearly singular, which is lesson 13-04's result. The tool goes at an even speed and the elbow has to
turn faster and faster to keep it going.

Measured, the fastest any joint has to turn to make each move in two seconds:

| move | joints straight | tool straight | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| short, near the middle | 0.27 rad/s | 0.31 rad/s | 1.1 |
| across the workspace | 0.51 | 0.87 | 1.7 |
| near full reach | 0.30 | 0.53 | 1.8 |
| through the middle | 0.59 | 0.83 | 1.4 |
| radially out to full reach | 0.82 | 3.39 | 4.2 |

A tool move always needs at least as much peak joint speed as the joint move between the same ends, because the joint
move is at a constant rate. The cost is modest away from the edge and 4.2 times at the radial move, for a tool going
at 0.17 m/s.

## Fix

Check the peak joint speed of the path against the joint's limit before sending it, which `peak_joint_speed` does, and
if it fails slow the move or change it. Slowing a Cartesian move to the joint limit is the usual answer, and it is the
tool that slows, not the joints, which is why a straight line near full extension is always slow. The turn between
poses is the short way round, so that a joint going from 3.1 to -3.1 has moved 0.08 radians and not 6.2.
