title: A consumer that spins to win latency and takes the machine
match: (spinning consumer|a whole core for latency|consumer used all of a core)
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 07-16-the-loop-measured

## Symptom

The latency numbers are the best they can be and everything else on the robot is slower. The vision pipeline loses frames, a fan runs
flat out, a battery drains, and a profiler shows one thread at 100 percent in a loop that does nothing.

## Cause

Spinning wins latency by never sleeping, and pays for it with the core.

Measured, 200 Hz for 2 seconds, the consumer thread's own CPU time:

| consumer | p50 | p99 | consumer CPU |
|---|---|---|---|
| spins | 0.4 us | 1.3 to 1.6 us | **1999 ms of 2000** |
| polls every 1 ms | 514 to 549 us | 1080 to 1146 us | 31 to 32 ms |
| sleeps on a condition | 39 to 65 us | 167 to 245 us | 4.7 to 8.9 ms |

The spinner is a thousand times faster at the median and used a whole core to do it, 400 times the CPU of the sleeper. On a machine
with spare cores that can be the right trade. On a robot with four cores and a vision pipeline it is not.

## Fix

Choose from the budget, not from the best number. A 200 Hz loop has 5000 us per cycle: the sleeper's p99 of 245 us is 5 percent of it,
the poller's 1100 us is 22 percent, and the spinner's 1.6 us is nothing but needs a core that nothing else may have. A 1 kHz loop with
a 1000 us budget cannot afford to poll at 1 ms at all.

If you must spin, give it a core of its own with CPU affinity and say so where the robot is configured, so that nothing else is
scheduled there and nobody is surprised.

Do not assert a spinner's loop count in a test. With every core busy the spinning thread is descheduled too, and goes round far less:
measured, an assertion that it went round 100 times per reading failed in 3 of 10 loaded runs. Print it; assert only what a slower
machine cannot break.
