## Symptom

With the cable out, one core is at full load, the log is a wall of "open failed",
and the machine's own device list is slow to update because the program is opening
a device that is not there tens of thousands of times a second. Or the opposite: the
cable is back and the robot sits there for a full minute before it notices.

## Cause

The reconnect has no wait, or the wrong wait. A cable is out for seconds or minutes
and the loop comes round in microseconds, so a retry with no pause is a try for every
tick for as long as the cable is out. A fixed pause is a compromise between two bad
answers, and whichever way it is set, one of them is bad.

Measured, a cable out for about a minute:

| wait between tries | tries while out | worst wait once back |
|---|---|---|
| every millisecond | 53267 | 0 ms |
| every 10 ms | 5929 | 4 ms |
| every second | 62 | 900 ms |
| 10 ms doubling to 2 s | 38 | 1952 ms |

The first two are prompt and hammer the system. The third is patient and slow. The
doubling is as patient as the third while it is out, **38 tries against 62**, and
quick for a glitch, because the first retries are 10, 20 and 40 ms. Its worst wait is
the cap, and the cap is the number to choose.

A subtler version of the same bug: the wait is computed as the initial value times
the factor to the power of the number of failures. For a long enough absence that
overflows to infinity, which then compares as no wait, or as a wait that never ends.

## Fix

Double the wait up to a cap, one step at a time, and never let it exceed the cap:

```cpp
double wait = start;
for (unsigned i = 0; i < attempt && wait < cap; ++i) wait *= grow;
return wait < cap ? wait : cap;
```

Start at no less than a millisecond, treat a factor under one as no growth, and make
the cap a promise: a million failures in a row, a robot left in a cupboard, is still
the cap and not infinity.
