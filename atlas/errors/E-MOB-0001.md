## Symptom

The hand misses its targets by a few millimetres in a direction that depends on where it is reaching, though the base and the arm are each well calibrated. It shows most on the far side of the reach and on long tools, and a target at the same place reached from a different base heading misses in a different direction.

## Cause

The arm's mount on the base was measured carefully in position and roughly in angle. A turn in the mount is multiplied by the distance out to the hand, like the base's heading: the grasp budget of 33-07 carries a mounting turn of 0.2 degree, which at 0.9 m is a few millimetres at the hand, and a mount turned by a whole degree would cost 15.7 mm there.

A position error of the mount is carried to the hand one for one; an angle error is not. It turns the whole arm about the mount, so its effect grows with the distance from the mount to the hand. A tape measure finds the first and cannot find the second, which is why the angle is the one usually left out.

## Fix

Measure the mount's angles as well as its offset, and put them in the mount transform used by every conversion.

```cpp
rc::mobile::MobileManipulator m;
m.mount = rc::arm::compose(rc::arm::trans(0.15, 0.0, 0.40), rc::arm::rot_z(mount_yaw));   // the angle measured, not assumed
const rc::arm::Pose arm_base = rc::mobile::arm_base_in_world(m, base);
```

Measuring an angle to a fraction of a degree needs a fixture or a calibration routine, not a ruler; the grasp budget of 33-07 says how small it has to be.
