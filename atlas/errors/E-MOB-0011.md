## Symptom

Following a line, the base lurches and moves far more than it needs to, while the arm does little. The base moves for small corrections that the arm alone could make.

## Cause

The base's rates were given the same price as the joints. With eight rates for six velocities there is a family of answers, and the solution chosen depends on what each rate costs. Driving a base is usually dearer than moving a joint.

With more rates than the hand's six velocities, the solver has a family of answers and picks the one that costs least. If base and joint rates cost the same, the base, which moves the hand furthest per unit of rate, does much of the work.

## Fix

Give the base's rates their own price in the weighted solution. A base 30 times as costly drove 1.20 m; one 33 times cheaper, 1.50 m.

```cpp
const std::vector<double> rates = rc::mobile::wb_rates(j, xi, 30.0, 0.05);   // w_base 30: the arm does more
```

The base's price is a design choice: a dear base keeps the arm doing the fine work and the base the travel.
