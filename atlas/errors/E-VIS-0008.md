## Symptom

An object found in the middle of the image is placed accurately on the floor, and the same object near the edge is
off by many centimetres, toward the centre. A straight wall or a line on the floor looks curved in the image, and a
line fitter finds a line with a large residual that grows towards the ends.

## Cause

A real lens is not a pinhole. It bends light more towards the edge of the picture than the middle, and the usual model
scales a point's distance from the centre by `1 + k1 r^2 + k2 r^4`. A wide lens has a negative `k1`, barrel distortion,
which pulls the edges in. The effect goes as a high power of the distance from the centre, so it is invisible in the middle
and large at the edge.

Measured, a lens with `k1 = -0.25` and `k2 = 0.05` on a 640 by 480 picture with a focal length of 500 pixels, how far a
point's pixel is from where a pinhole would put it:

| normalised radius | pinhole | with the lens | moved |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 50.0 px | 49.9 px | 0.1 px |
| 0.2 | 100.0 | 99.0 | 1.0 |
| 0.4 | 200.0 | 192.3 | 7.7 |
| 0.6 | 300.0 | 274.9 | 25.1 |
| 0.8 | 400.0 | 344.2 | 55.8 |

At the corner that is 56 pixels, and at two metres a pixel is 4 mm: **22.3 cm** on the floor. A straight edge one metre above
the axis bowed by 15.9 pixels off the line through its ends.

## Fix

Undo the lens before using a pixel as a direction: `unproject` takes a pixel to the direction it looked along. After it, the
same straight edge was level to 1.8e-11 of a pixel. And calibrate the coefficients, which are not on a datasheet: they are
found by photographing a pattern whose geometry is known, and the focal length and centre come out of the same fit.
