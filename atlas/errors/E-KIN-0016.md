## Symptom

The feedforward makes tracking worse, or better at low speed and worse at high. The
model was typed from a textbook, checked at rest where it agrees with the holding
torque, and every pose tried looked sensible. It is wrong only where the arm is
moving fast.

## Cause

Gravity and inertia can be checked at rest. The velocity terms cannot: they are zero
there. A sign wrong in one of them, or a term left out, is invisible to every static
test and to a slow move, where they are small.

There is a check that needs no second model. With no motor torque and no friction
nothing adds energy to the arm or takes it away, so a free swing must keep its total
of kinetic and potential energy. That holds only if the inertia, the weight and the
velocity terms agree with one another, and the energy is computed from the
positions of the masses, by a route that shares no algebra with the torque formula.

Measured, five seconds of a free swing from rest, which reaches 38 rad/s at a joint:

| model | drift in the total energy, 10.5 J at the start |
|---|---|
| correct | 1.5e-06 J |
| both velocity terms with the wrong sign | 26.6 J |
| the elbow's squared velocity term left out | 151.8 J |

The wrong ones create more energy than the arm started with.

## Fix

Test the model against energy, with a free swing hard enough that the velocity terms
matter, and assert the drift is tiny. And test the mass matrix against the kinetic
energy computed from the positions of the masses, as a second independent route: the
three velocities (1, 0), (0, 1) and (1, 1) give all three entries.

A model that passes both can be trusted to be fed forward. One that was only checked at
rest cannot.
