## Symptom

The arm stops short of its target, further when it is stretched out, and more at the shoulder than at the wrist. Raising the gain shrinks the error and makes the joint shake.

## Cause

A PD controller with nothing else holds the arm up with its error: the droop is the weight's torque over the gain. It drooped 0.1113 rad, against the 0.1093 that the weight over the gain predicts.

A PD controller makes torque only from an error. To hold up a weight it needs a steady torque, so it needs a steady error: the weight's torque over the proportional gain. Stretched poses have the largest gravity torque and so the largest droop.

## Fix

Add the model's gravity torque as a feedforward. With it the error was 0.000000000 rad.

```cpp
const std::vector<double> tau = rc::arm::pd_torque(model, q, qd, target, gains, true);   // compensate
```

Raising the gain only shrinks the droop, and costs stability.
