## Symptom

Two separate things from the same cause, that a pixel is a coarse thing. A lens correction that fixes the middle of the
image and leaves the corners visibly wrong. And a distance estimate from the size of a target that is good at a metre and
useless at ten.

## Cause

**The inverse of the lens model is not another formula.** Projection scales a point by a factor that depends on its radius, and
undoing it needs the radius of the point that is being looked for. It is found by guessing that the point is where the pixel
says, working out the factor there, correcting, and repeating. Each pass is nearer, and the corner, where the bend is
largest, needs the most.

Measured, the corner pixel of the lens above, the error of the direction after `n` passes:

| passes | error |
|---|---|
| 1 | 14.5 px |
| 2 | 3.94 px |
| 3 | 1.08 px |
| 5 | 0.083 px |
| 10 | 0.000134 px |
| 20 | 3.5e-10 px |

One pass, which is what a quick correction does, is out by 14.5 pixels at the corner. The other quick fix, applying the lens
model again with its coefficients negated, which looks like an inverse and is not, is out by 18.9.

**A distance from a target's size is a division by a number of pixels.** For a target 0.2 m wide and a width measured one pixel
too wide:

| true distance | appears | error |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 m | 100.0 px | -0.010 m |
| 2.0 m | 50.0 px | -0.039 m |
| 5.0 m | 20.0 px | -0.238 m |
| 10.0 m | 10.0 px | -0.909 m |

The target gets smaller as it recedes and the pixel does not, so the error goes as the square of the distance. A focal length two
percent out makes every distance two percent out.

## Fix

Iterate the inverse until it has converged, which the test checks to a ten thousandth of a pixel at ten passes and to 3.5e-10 at
twenty. And do not trust a range from size beyond the distance at which a pixel is a tolerable fraction of the target: plan around
the range at which the width is a hundred pixels, not ten.
