title: A latency report that only counted the readings that got through
match: (readings were dropped and not counted|survivor|latency of the ones that got through)
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 07-16-the-loop-measured

## Symptom

The latency report is excellent and the robot misses things. A consumer that falls behind looks, in the numbers, no worse than one
that keeps up. The 99th percentile is flat as load rises.

## Cause

A measurement of the readings that were *delivered* says nothing about the ones that were not. A full queue refuses a reading. If the
refusal is silent, the histogram holds only survivors, and the survivors of an overloaded system are the lucky ones.

Measured, a 5 ms producer against a consumer that takes 20 ms a reading, with a queue of 4:

```
    produced 60, delivered 19, dropped 41, slots missed by the producer 0
```

**41 of 60 readings were lost.** The 19 that got through were taken within about four readings of being made, because the queue
never held more than four: a perfectly respectable latency distribution for a consumer that was throwing away two thirds of its input.

## Fix

Keep the books. Every reading produced is delivered or dropped, and the two add up:

```cpp
RC_CHECK_EQ(report.produced, report.delivered + report.dropped);
```

That conservation law is the first thing the suite checks, in every configuration, because without it any latency figure is a figure
about whatever got through. Report the drop count beside the percentiles, always, and treat a nonzero count as a finding.

Also check that a slow consumer does not slow the producer. Measured: the producer here kept its rate, 60 readings and 0 missed
slots, which is what a drop policy is for. A producer that blocks on a full queue has moved the loss from the queue to the sensor.
