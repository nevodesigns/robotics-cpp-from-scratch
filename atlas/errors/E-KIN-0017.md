## Symptom

A force controller or a payload estimate that is right on the shoulder and wrong on the elbow,
sometimes in sign, so that the arm pushes into the thing it should be holding off. It looks
right for forces along one axis and wrong for others, and it passes a check with a single
easy pose.

## Cause

The Jacobian of lesson 13-04 maps joint speeds to tool speed: `v = J * qdot`. Torque is the
other way round, and it is the transpose, not the same matrix. Power is the same at both ends
of a rigid arm, so torque dotted with joint speed equals force dotted with tool speed. Since
tool speed is `J * qdot`, that gives `tau = J^T * F`.

Written with `J` instead of `J^T` the first row is the same and the second is not, which is why
it survives a casual look. Measured, holding 10 N along x at a pose of (0.4, 1.1):

| | shoulder | elbow |
|---|---|---|
| with the transpose | -5.94 N m | -3.99 N m |
| with the matrix as it is | -5.94 N m | **+4.89 N m** |

The shoulder is identical and the elbow has the wrong sign.

## Fix

Check it against something that shares no algebra with it: the principle of virtual work. Move
each joint a little, see how far the tool goes, and the torque at that joint is the work the force
does per radian. The test does that numerically across 1014 poses and forces and agrees to 1.9e-09
N m, which no untransposed matrix could.

```cpp
load.shoulder = j.a * force.fx + j.c * force.fy;
load.elbow    = j.b * force.fx + j.d * force.fy;
```
