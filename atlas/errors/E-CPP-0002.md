title: Assignment used where comparison was meant

## Symptom

A condition is always true, or a variable changes value unexpectedly inside an
if statement. The compiler may warn, or may say nothing at all.

## Cause

A single equals sign assigns a value. Two equals signs compare. Writing
if (x = 5) sets x to five and then treats five, a non zero value, as true. It is
valid C++, which is why it is not always an error.

## Fix

Use == to compare and = to assign. Build with warnings enabled, which this
repository does by default, so the compiler points at the ones it can see. When
you genuinely mean to assign inside a condition, wrap it in extra parentheses so
the intent is visible to both the compiler and the next reader.
