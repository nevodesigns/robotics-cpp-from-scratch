title: An angle clamped as though it were a number
match: clamping an angle is not clamping a number
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 13-05-the-angle-it-cannot-hold

## Symptom

A joint limit check rejects poses the arm can obviously hold. The arm is sitting
at 1.0 rad, well inside its range, and the solver's answer for a target it is
almost touching comes back refused.

Or the opposite: asked to point somewhere behind its stop, the arm swings the
long way round to the far end of its travel when the near end was a third of the
distance away.

Printing the angle shows something like -6.10, or 3.28, or -3.00.

## Cause

An angle is a direction, not a quantity, and the two are only the same while
nothing wraps.

`std::atan2` answers in (-pi, pi]. The closed form solver builds the shoulder
angle from a difference of two of them, so its output covers roughly
(-2pi, 2pi). The angle -6.10 and the angle 0.18 are the same direction and the
same physical pose, and a joint that can hold one holds the other. A comparison
written as

```cpp
if (angle < limit.lo || angle > limit.hi) reject();
```

refuses -6.10 against a limit of [-0.6, 2.2] even though the arm is already
sitting there.

The clamp has the same defect with a louder result. Measured on that shoulder,
across six illegal directions, clamping as a number picked the further end three
times:

| wanted | as number | as angle | number off | angle off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| -3.000 | -0.600 | 2.200 | 2.400 | 1.083 |
| -2.600 | -0.600 | 2.200 | 2.000 | 1.483 |
| -2.200 | -0.600 | -0.600 | 1.600 | 1.600 |
| -6.100 | -0.600 | 0.183 | 0.783 | 0.000 |

The last row is the sharpest. -6.10 is legal, exactly, and the number clamp
moved it 0.78 rad to a stop.

## Fix

**Fold to the same direction before comparing.** One `fmod` and one conditional:

```cpp
double folded = limit.lo + std::fmod(angle - limit.lo, kTurn);
if (folded < limit.lo) folded += kTurn;
return folded <= limit.hi;
```

**Measure the distance between two angles as an angle.** `to - from` is not the
answer when either has wrapped. Bring the difference into [-pi, pi] and the
comparison means what it says. That function belongs in the same header as the
limit, because every use of one needs the other.

**Keep the number clamp, under a name that says so.** Clamping into a range is
correct for a voltage, a duty cycle, a velocity, a setpoint in metres. The bug
is not the clamp, it is using it on a quantity that wraps. Two functions with
honest names cost nothing and make the wrong one hard to reach for.

**Treat a joint that can turn a full revolution as unlimited.** A continuous
joint with a span of 2pi or more has no illegal direction, and the fold will
happily return an angle that satisfies the comparison by accident. Answering
that case first is one line and removes a whole class of confusion.
