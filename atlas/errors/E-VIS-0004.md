## Symptom

Vision that works in the lab and fails on the shop floor, or works in the
morning and fails at midday, or works until somebody turns on the overhead
lights. It does not degrade: it finds the marker perfectly and then finds the
entire frame.

Adjusting the threshold fixes it for that lighting and breaks a different one.

## Cause

A constant threshold is a claim about how bright the scene is, and lighting is
the one thing about a scene guaranteed to change.

A marker at 200 on a background at 40, as the lights come up:

| extra light | background | marker | fixed 128 finds | Otsu finds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 40 | 200 | 20 px | 20 px |
| 40 | 80 | 240 | 20 px | 20 px |
| 80 | 120 | 255 | 20 px | 20 px |
| 120 | **160** | 255 | **200 px** | 20 px |

The marker is 20 pixels. At the last row the background itself is above 128, so
everything passes the test and the "marker" is the whole picture, whose centre
is the middle of the frame, which is where the robot then goes.

Note the first three rows. It worked, exactly, three times. That is how it
ships.

## Fix

Take the threshold from the picture.

```cpp
const int threshold = rc::vision::otsu_threshold(rc::vision::histogram_of(image));
```

Otsu's method tries every threshold and keeps the one that puts the most
variance between the two groups it makes. It is one pass over 256 histogram
buckets, which costs nothing next to reading the frame, and it found exactly the
marker at every exposure above.

Three things that go with it.

**It answers where to split, not whether to.** On a picture with no two groups
in it the split is meaningless, and the failure is not that it finds nothing.
See `E-VIS-0006`.

**Control the lighting where you can.** An automatic threshold is a defence
against variation, not a substitute for a scene you designed: a marker chosen to
be much brighter or much darker than anything else in the frame makes both the
threshold and its failures obvious.

**Use the colour if the marker has one.** A red marker against a grey factory is
a far better signal than a bright one against a bright floor, and thresholding
one channel, or a difference of two, is the same code with a better input.

And test at more than one exposure. Every fixed threshold passes the test that
was written at the exposure it was tuned at.
