## Symptom

A name that certainly exists does not exist, and often several at once:

```text
solution.hpp:17:15: error: 'sqrt' is not a member of 'std'
solution.hpp:22:15: error: 'sqrt' is not a member of 'std'
solution.hpp:27:15: error: 'fabs' is not a member of 'std'
```

Three errors, one mistake.

## Cause

The standard library is not one thing you get automatically. It is a set of
headers, and a name only exists in a file that included the header declaring it.
`std::sqrt` needs `<cmath>`. `std::vector` needs `<vector>`. `std::string` needs
`<string>`, and `std::cout` needs `<iostream>`.

This is the mistake that produces the most errors per line of cause, because a
header is usually used in several places. Measured in lesson 00-03, one missing
`#include <cmath>` produced three of the file's six errors, on both GCC and
Clang, and adding one line removed all three.

It also cascades sideways. A missing `<vector>` gives "vector is not a member of
std", and then every later use of the variable that was going to be a vector
gives "was not declared in this scope", so one forgotten line can produce seven
or eight errors of two different shapes.

There is one important variation. If the name is `span`, `format` or `expected`,
the header is not missing: those arrived after C++17 and this curriculum
compiles at C++17. See `E-CPP-0024`.

## Fix

Read the `note:` line. GCC prints the answer directly underneath:

```text
note: 'std::sqrt' is defined in header '<cmath>';
      did you forget to '#include <cmath>'?
```

That line is skipped constantly, because it does not say "error" and it appears
in the middle of a wall of text. It is frequently the whole fix.

Then rebuild before reading anything else. Errors that were consequences of this
one disappear on their own, and what is left is a shorter list of real problems.

Include what you use, in the file that uses it. Relying on a header that happens
to include another one works until somebody tidies that header, and then the
failure appears in your file, which you did not change.
