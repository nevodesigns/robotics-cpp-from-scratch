## Symptom

A struct that gets one `alignas(16)` or one SIMD vector member and is suddenly three times its old size, and an array of them correspondingly bigger. Two
bytes of tags around a vector of four floats take 48 bytes.

## Cause

An over-aligned member sits at a multiple of its alignment, so a gap of up to 15 bytes opens before it, and the whole struct takes the alignment of its most
strictly aligned member, so it is padded at the end to a multiple of 16 as well.

Measured, a byte, a 16 byte aligned vector of four floats, and a byte: eighteen bytes of data, **48 bytes** of struct. The vector lands at offset 16, the last
byte at 32, and the end is padded to 48. The struct's own alignment is 16, which `layout_of` reports as the largest of its members'.

## Fix

Be deliberate about it. Alignment is a request to the hardware for faster access, and each use costs in padding. Group the over-aligned members first, so no
gap opens before them: the same ordering rule as for any struct. And check `sizeof` and `alignof` with a `static_assert` where a layout is part of a contract,
so a change to a member's alignment is a compile error and not a surprise in a log file.
