title: Two arm postures that become indistinguishable
match: two answers that become one
platforms: linux, windows

## Symptom

An arm that flips. Asked for a sequence of nearby points, it drives to the first
with the elbow up and the second with the elbow down, swinging through a large
motion to reach a target a millimetre away. Sometimes it does it once and never
again, and the same command replayed does something different.

Nothing reports an error, because both postures put the tool exactly where it
was asked to go.

## Cause

Inverse kinematics usually has more than one answer, and near a singularity the
answers converge.

The elbow up and elbow down solutions for the same tool position, as the arm
extends:

| reach | elbow up | elbow down | apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.50000 | 1.982313 | -1.982313 | **3.964626** |
| 0.80000 | 0.958192 | -0.958192 | 1.916384 |
| 0.88000 | 0.425094 | -0.425094 | 0.850188 |
| 0.89990 | 0.030000 | -0.030000 | 0.060001 |
| 0.89999 | 0.009487 | -0.009487 | **0.018974** |

Two completely different postures, four radians apart with the arm half out and
two hundredths of a radian apart near the limit.

At that separation the choice is decided by a rounding error, a sensor's last
bit, or which way a solver happened to step first. Both answers remain exactly
correct, so nothing in the solver has anything to complain about, and the arm
swings through half its workspace to satisfy a request that moved the tool by a
millimetre.

## Fix

Choose the posture deliberately and keep it.

**Pick the solution nearest the current one.** The arm is already somewhere; the
answer closest to where it is, is almost always the one meant, and it is one
comparison.

```cpp
const auto solutions = rc::kin::solve(x, y, l1, l2);
// then take the branch whose joints are closest to the joints you have
```

**Carry the branch as state.** Elbow up or elbow down is a decision about the
task, not about the point: reaching into a shelf and reaching over an obstacle
want different ones, and the branch should be chosen once for the motion rather
than recomputed per point.

**Reject a solution that requires a large joint move for a small tool move.**
The ratio is computable before anything turns, and a limit on it catches this,
a singularity, and a solver bug with one test.

**And plan away from the boundary.** Two solutions being close together is the
same condition as a joint rate being large, which is `E-KIN-0005`, and staying a
measured distance inside the workspace fixes both.
