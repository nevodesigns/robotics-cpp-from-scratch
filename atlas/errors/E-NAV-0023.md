## Symptom

A robot told to drive a square comes back to a point that is not where it started, by a lot, and the odometry
says it closed. The error is the same every time: it is a systematic one, in the same direction, and no
amount of averaging helps. Lesson 16-01 measured what each error costs once and found the wheelbase was the
worst. This is that, for a robot that has all of them at once.

## Cause

Odometry believes three numbers it has not measured: that each wheel travels the distance its encoder says,
and that the wheels are the distance apart the drawing says. Tyres wear and inflate unevenly and the contact
patch is not where the drawing puts it. Each is a percent or two out, and they add.

Measured, a robot told to drive a square four metres on a side, 16 metres in all, with the odometry believing
the drawing:

| the real robot | ends up from where it thinks | heading error |
|---|---|---|
| left wheel 1.2 % big | 132.2 cm | 25.3 deg |
| right wheel 0.6 % small | 75.2 cm | 14.8 deg |
| wheel base 2 % wider | 35.4 cm | 7.1 deg |
| both wheels 1 % big | 17.8 cm | 3.6 deg |
| all of them together | 249.9 cm | 46.4 deg |

Two wheels that are equally big do not veer, but they carry the robot one percent further on a straight and
turn it one percent further on a spin: four ninety degree turns, each 0.9 of a degree long, are 3.6 degrees
round by the end. Wheels that differ make every straight line a curve, and that is what puts the left wheel
at a quarter of a turn of heading error.

## Fix

Measure the three. `E-NAV-0024` says how, in two moves, and `E-NAV-0025` what the measurement can and cannot
give. Do not tune the numbers until the square closes: three numbers fitted to one path are right for that
path.
