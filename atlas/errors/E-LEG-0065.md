title: A controller that does not know the bound is coming
match: the balance controller wasted the wide window and had nothing in the narrow one
match: the recovery was slow in a trot and fast in a walk
match: when the bound on the ZMP changes with the support, the predictive controller recovers from pushes as large as a clipped fixed gain law does, and settles sooner
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 36-05-looking-ahead-with-the-body
---

## Symptom

The body recovers from a push slowly, or overshoots when the support narrows as the swing feet land. A clipped fixed gain controller behaves as if it is always surprised by its own feet.

## Cause

A fixed gain law sees only the bound on the zero moment point that is available now. In a trot the bound changes in a known way: here 15 cm for 0.2 s and 1 cm for 0.2 s, alternately, as the swing feet land. A law that cannot see the narrow time coming spends the wide time badly. For a push of 0.6 m/s the body was back at the middle after 1.05 s with the fixed law (gains 3.0 and 0.9, clipped) and after 0.65 s with the predictive controller.

## Fix

Give the controller the schedule of the bound over its horizon. Expect it to be quicker, not stronger: the largest push recovered was 0.70 m/s by the fixed law and 0.71 m/s by the predictive controller, against 0.90 m/s if the bound were always wide.

```cpp
double bound[15];
for (int k = 0; k < 15; ++k) bound[k] = gait.zmp_bound(t + k * 0.05);   // 0.15 or 0.01 m
const double u = rc::legged::mpc_zmp(x, v, h, 0.05, 15, bound, 1.0, 1.0, 0.01, 300, nullptr);
```

For a small push the fixed law was the quieter, 0.05 s against 0.40 s: planning has a cost when there is nothing to plan for.
