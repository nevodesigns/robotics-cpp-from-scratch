id: E-NUM-0020
title: A tolerance that is loose at one magnitude and impossible at another
match: one absolute tolerance means two different things
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 01-08-close-enough
---

## Symptom

A comparison with a tolerance behaves differently in different parts of the
same program. Near the origin it accepts values that are visibly wrong. Out at
range, the same function rejects two numbers that are as close as two numbers
can be, and a loop waiting for them to agree never finishes.

```cpp
if (std::fabs(a - b) <= 1e-9) { /* close enough */ }
```

## Cause

The tolerance is an absolute distance, and the numbers it is comparing are not.

Doubles are not spread evenly along the number line. The gap between
neighbours grows with magnitude, so a fixed distance covers a different number
of representable values depending on where you are standing.

| magnitude | gap to next | doubles 1e-9 spans | adjacent pair within 1e-9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1e0 | 2.220e-16 | 4.504e+06 | yes |
| 1e3 | 1.137e-13 | 8.796e+03 | yes |
| 1e6 | 1.164e-10 | 8.590e+00 | yes |
| 1e9 | 1.192e-07 | 8.389e-03 | **no** |
| 1e12 | 1.221e-04 | 8.192e-06 | **no** |

Both ends are wrong, in opposite directions.

At magnitude 1 the tolerance spans four and a half million distinct doubles, so
it is loose, not strict.

At magnitude 1e9 it cannot reach the next representable double. There is
nothing between a value and its neighbour, so no two *different* numbers can
ever be within 1e-9 of each other out there. The tolerance is not strict, it is
unsatisfiable, and an algorithm that iterates until two values agree to 1e-9
can only stop if it lands on the identical bit pattern.

## Fix

**Make the tolerance relative.** Scale it by the size of the numbers and it
means the same thing everywhere. Measured, a relative 1e-9 spans between
4.5e6 and 8.8e6 doubles at every magnitude from 1 to 1e12, a factor of two
across twelve orders of magnitude.

```cpp
difference <= relative * std::fmax(std::fabs(a), std::fabs(b))
```

**Keep an absolute floor as well, for zero.** A relative tolerance has nothing
to be relative to near zero, so `1e-18` and `2e-18` come out a hundred percent
apart and nothing is ever close to zero. `rc::core::nearly_equal` takes both and
passes if either does.

**Read a tolerance in representable doubles before trusting it.**
`rc::core::ulps_between` turns "is 1e-9 reasonable here" into a number. Under
one means impossible; millions means loose.

**Use `fmax` of the two magnitudes, not one of them.** Dividing by `a` makes
the answer depend on argument order, so `nearly_equal(a, b)` and
`nearly_equal(b, a)` can disagree.
