title: A move in the joints assumed to be a move in a line
match: moving the joints in a straight line moves the tool in a curve, by centimetres
match: expected this to be true: strays > 0.010
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 13-09-straight-in-space-or-in-the-joints
---

## Symptom

A tool that should travel along a straight edge, a weld seam, a pick from a tray, a wipe across a surface,
bows away from the line in the middle and comes back at the end. The ends are exactly right, which is why the
motion passed a check of the start and the finish, and the middle is off by centimetres.

## Cause

Both joints were told to go from where they were to where they are going, in a straight line in angle, and to
arrive together. That is the easiest move there is: it needs no inverse kinematics and cannot fail. But the
tool's position is a trigonometric function of the angles, so a straight line in angle is a curve in space.

Measured, a 0.5 m and 0.4 m arm, five moves of two seconds each:

| move | length | the tool strays from the line |
|---|---|---|
| short, near the middle | 0.320 m | 22.7 mm |
| across the workspace | 0.700 m | 83.6 mm |
| near full reach | 0.500 m | 37.3 mm |
| through the middle | 0.800 m | 121.1 mm |
| radially out to full reach | 0.345 m | 12.7 mm |

The worst, 121 mm, is on a move of 0.8 m: a seventh of its length. Even the shortest strays by more than two
centimetres.

## Fix

Say which move is wanted. If only the ends matter, the joint move is right and it is the cheapest, with the
lowest peak joint speed there is for those ends. If the path matters, move the tool in a line and solve the joint
angles at every point along it, which `cartesian_path` does. Measure the deviation as the largest perpendicular
distance of the tool from the line, which `path_deviation` computes, and put a number on how straight is straight
enough for the task.
