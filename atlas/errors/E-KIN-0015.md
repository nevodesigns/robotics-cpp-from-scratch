id: E-KIN-0015
title: Independent joint control of a coupled arm
match: feeding the inverse dynamics forward tracks a fast move that gravity alone cannot
match: expected this to be true: gravity_only.worst_mm
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 13-07-what-it-takes-to-move-it
---

## Symptom

A slow move is accurate and a fast one is not, by a large and repeatable amount, in a
shape that looks like a lag and a swing. The tracking error grows with speed and
with how bent the elbow is. Raising the gains helps a little and eventually makes the
arm shake.

An elbow told to hold still is dragged off its position when the shoulder swings, and
comes back afterwards.

## Cause

Each joint was given its own controller, gravity was fed forward, and everything
else was left to the feedback to correct. Everything else is big: the inertia the
motor must accelerate, and the terms that come from one joint's motion acting on the
other, the Coriolis and centripetal terms. A swinging shoulder pulls the elbow
outward.

Measured, a shoulder swing of 1.0 rad in 0.4 s with the elbow moving 1.4 rad, gains of
80 and 14, ten times what a slow move needs:

| feedforward | worst tool error | rms tool error |
|---|---|---|
| gravity only | 281.9 mm | 95.6 mm |
| gravity and inverse dynamics | 6.1 mm | 2.0 mm |

And with the elbow told to stay put, how far the swinging shoulder pulled it:

| feedforward | elbow strayed |
|---|---|
| gravity only | 0.077 rad |
| gravity and inverse dynamics | 0.000 rad |

## Fix

Compute the torque the motion needs, from the desired trajectory, and add it:

```cpp
const JointTorques feed = inverse_dynamics(arm, inertia, d1, d2, w1, w2, z1, z2, payload);
shoulder = kp * (d1 - q1) + kd * (w1 - v1) + feed.shoulder;
```

The feedback is then left to correct only what the model got wrong, which is small.
The trajectory has to supply the acceleration, so it has to be a smooth one with a
continuous acceleration, a quintic and not a ramp. The model has to be right, which
is `E-KIN-0016`.
