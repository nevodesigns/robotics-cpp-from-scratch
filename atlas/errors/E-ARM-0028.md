## Symptom

The model's gravity torques do not hold the arm still, or hold it in some poses and let it drift in others. The arm drifts in some poses under its own gravity compensation, and holds in others.

## Cause

A sign, or the order of a cross product, is wrong in the recursion. The Newton-Euler recursion has many such places, and a mistake in one gives torques that look reasonable and are wrong. Two things that are true catch it: the torque of gravity is the slope of the potential energy (it matched to 0.00000001 N m), and a uniform rod of mass m and length L held out level needs m g L over 2 (3.924 N m).

The recursion passes velocities and forces from link to link through many cross products, and one wrong order or sign gives torques of the right size in the wrong place. They are hard to see by looking. A test against a physical truth catches what reading the code does not.

## Fix

Test the model against those truths, and against a simulated free fall, before any controller uses it.

```cpp
const std::vector<double> g = rc::arm::gravity_torques(model, q);
// compare with (energy(q + h e_i) - energy(q - h e_i)) / (2 h) for each joint i
```
