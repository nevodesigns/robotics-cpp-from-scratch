## Symptom

Damped least squares was added because the joint rates were exploding, and it
worked: nothing trips any more. Now the tool does not follow the path near full
extension. It slows, lags, cuts the corner, and arrives late somewhere slightly
different, and nothing anywhere reports a tracking error.

Turning the damping down brings back the lurching.

## Cause

Damping is not a fix for a singularity, it is a decision about what to give up
there.

Instead of solving `J q = v` exactly, damped least squares minimises the tool
error and the joint effort together, with lambda setting the exchange rate. The
matrix it inverts is `J J' + lambda^2 I`, which cannot be singular, so there is
always an answer. The answer is not the motion you asked for.

Measured a tenth of a millimetre from full extension, asked for 0.1 m/s outward:

| lambda | q1 rate | q2 rate | speed given |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.000 | 6.67 | -15.00 | 0.10000 |
| 0.001 | 6.49 | -14.61 | 0.09738 |
| 0.010 | 1.80 | -4.06 | **0.02707** |
| 0.050 | 0.10 | -0.22 | 0.00149 |
| 0.200 | 0.01 | -0.01 | 0.00028 |

Every step of damping costs joint rate and costs speed, together. At lambda
0.01 the arm moves at a quarter of the rate and delivers a quarter of the speed.

That is the correct behaviour, and it is why a real machine slows to a crawl
near full extension rather than throwing itself at the stop. It is not tracking
the path, and something has to know that.

## Fix

Treat damping as a speed limit and account for it.

**Leave it switched on.** Away from a singularity it costs almost nothing:
measured at half reach with lambda 0.01, the tool still moves at over 99 percent
of the speed asked for. So there is no need to switch it in and out, which is
one fewer mode to get wrong.

**Report the shortfall.** The difference between the velocity asked for and the
velocity the joints will actually deliver is computable, `J q` against `v`, and
it is the number that tells a path follower it is falling behind rather than
tracking.

**Scale the whole path, not one axis.** Slowing one direction and not the others
distorts the shape being drawn. A time scaling that slows everything preserves
it, and is what a trajectory generator does with this information.

**Choose lambda from the joint rate you can afford**, not by feel. The table
above is one loop and the arm's rated speed is on its data sheet, so the
selection is a measurement rather than a preference.

And keep the distance measure alongside it. `manipulability` says how close the
trouble is, damping decides what to do about it, and reporting both makes the
behaviour explicable to whoever watches the arm slow down.
