## Symptom

The robot plans confidently through space no sensor has ever seen: round a blind
corner, past the edge of a loading dock, into a stairwell, through a doorway
that turns out to be glass.

The map looks right. Everything the robot has actually seen is correct. The
route goes through the part it has not.

## Cause

The map has two answers and the world needs three.

A grid of booleans, or of floats initialised to zero, has to call unlooked-at
ground something. Initialised to free, the planner treats every unseen cell as
drivable. Initialised to occupied, the robot will not leave its starting square,
so somebody changes it to free and the problem is back.

The absence of evidence is its own state, and it is the state that most of the
world is in at any moment.

The same mistake appears twice more in a scan pipeline:

- **Marking the whole ray free, endpoint included.** The beam stopped on
  something and the code erases it in the same pass. Only the endpoint gets the
  other answer.
- **Marking the endpoint occupied on a max-range return.** A beam that ran out
  without hitting anything says the space was empty and says nothing at all
  about its far end. Marking it occupied paints a ring of phantom wall at
  exactly max range around every place the robot has ever stood.

## Fix

Three states, and a planner that knows the difference.

```cpp
enum class Cell { unknown, free, occupied };
```

Represent them with evidence rather than with a flag: log odds at zero is
unknown, and a cell needs enough observations to move past a threshold before
the map will commit. That makes "one reading from a sensor right seven times in
ten" the non-answer it actually is, and it makes evidence that cancels return to
not knowing rather than to whichever reading arrived last.

Then decide, explicitly and in one place, what unknown means for the caller:

- **A planner** should treat unknown as blocked, or as expensive, and should say
  which. Frontier exploration is the deliberate exception, and it is deliberate.
- **A safety check** must treat unknown as occupied. There is no argument for
  driving into ground nothing has looked at.
- **A display** should draw unknown as its own colour. An operator looking at a
  map where unseen and empty are the same grey cannot tell whether the robot is
  confident or blind.

Pass whether the beam stopped on something into the update, so a max-range
reading can mark its ray free without inventing an obstacle at the end of it.
