id: E-RT-0002
title: Hunting an allocation with a stopwatch
match: a stopwatch cannot find an allocation on a general purpose machine
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 07-04-the-allocation-you-did-not-see
---

## Symptom

A control loop occasionally misses its deadline. Somebody times the loop body,
finds the worst case is forty microseconds against a median of a quarter of one,
and goes looking for what takes forty microseconds.

Days later the answer is that nothing does. The timing gets instrumented more
finely, which makes it noisier, and the search moves to the next suspect.

## Cause

On a general purpose operating system the loudest thing in a timing measurement
is the operating system.

Measured over a hundred thousand iterations of two loop bodies, one of which
allocates and one of which cannot:

| loop body | p50 | p99 | p99.9 | worst |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a fixed array | 0.250 us | 0.300 | 0.450 | **60.376** |
| a vector each tick | 4.050 us | 6.050 | 18.000 | 36.044 |

The body that touches nothing but stack has a worst case two hundred and forty
times its own median, and a worse worst case than the body that allocates. That
sixty microseconds is a scheduling event, a page fault, an interrupt: the
machine, not the code.

So no timing threshold separates "this allocated" from "the kernel was busy".
The allocation is real and it is a hundred times quieter than the room.

## Fix

Do not listen for it. Forbid it, and assert the count.

```cpp
#include <rc/test/leak_check.hpp>

rc::test::AllocationCount count;
loop_body();
RC_CHECK(count.none());
```

That is deterministic, identical on every toolchain, and it fails on the line
that allocated rather than somewhere downstream. It is also a different question
from a leak: a loop that allocates and frees a hundred blocks per tick leaks
nothing and is still unfit to run at 500 Hz, and `LeakCheck` cannot see it
because its counters come back to where they started.

Two things go with it.

**Make it a test, not an investigation.** An allocation-free loop is a property,
so assert it once and it stays asserted. Finding it again next year with a
profiler is the expensive way.

**Keep timing for what timing is good at.** The histogram from lesson 07-03
answers "does this loop meet its deadline on this machine", which is a real
question with a real answer. It does not answer "why", and the numbers above are
why.

What the counter usually finds is `E-RT-0003`.
