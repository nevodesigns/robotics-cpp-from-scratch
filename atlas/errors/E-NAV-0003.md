title: A filter that has stopped listening
match: predicting adds uncertainty, and correcting takes it away

## Symptom

An estimate that ignores its sensors. Measurements arrive, they disagree with
the estimate by metres, and the estimate barely moves.

It usually works at first. The filter tracks well for a while, then gradually
stops responding, and by the time anybody notices it is asserting a position
with great confidence and no evidence.

Turning the sensor off entirely changes nothing, which is the clue.

## Cause

The prediction step moved the estimate without adding any uncertainty:

```cpp
void predict(double motion) {
  estimate_.value += motion;      // and nothing else
}
```

Every correction reduces the variance. If nothing ever increases it, the
variance falls monotonically toward zero, and the gain toward new measurements
falls with it:

```text
gain = prior_variance / (prior_variance + measurement_variance)
```

A prior variance near zero gives a gain near zero, and a gain near zero means
the estimate does not move. The filter has concluded it is certain, and nothing
can talk it out of that.

The physical statement it is making is that motion is perfect: that the robot
went exactly as far as the odometer said. Lesson 16-01 measured what that is
worth.

## Fix

Add the uncertainty the motion introduced:

```cpp
void predict(double motion) {
  estimate_.value += motion;
  estimate_.variance += motion_variance_;
}
```

`motion_variance_` is the filter's opinion of its own odometry, per step. It is
what stops the variance collapsing, and it is what lets a fresh measurement
matter again after the robot has moved.

Two ways to see this failing before it costs anything.

**Watch the variance, not just the value.** A filter is two numbers and the
second one is the interesting one. A variance that only ever falls is broken
whatever the position looks like.

**Test that a filter with no measurements gets less sure.** Predict a thousand
times, feed it nothing, and require the variance to have grown substantially. A
filter that is more certain after a thousand blind steps than it was at the
start has the bug, and the test is three lines.

The opposite mistake is worth knowing too. A motion variance far larger than the
truth makes the filter distrust its own prediction and chase every measurement,
which is noisy rather than confidently wrong, and much easier to spot. See
`E-NAV-0004`.
