title: A rise attempted with no check of the torque
match: the robot tried to stand with a heavy payload and stalled
match: the knees could not lift it from a crouch
match: the knee's torque is greatest at the bottom of the rise and falls as the legs straighten
match: a 12 kg robot can rise, a slow rise uses more of the knee's thermal life than a fast one, and the heaviest bodies are limited by torque and by heat
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 36-03-getting-up
---

## Symptom

A heavy robot tries to rise, strains at the bottom of the movement and either stalls or gets halfway and drops. The same robot rises easily without its payload.

## Cause

No one checked the torque the rise needs before trying it. The knee carries its share of the weight times how far the shank leans from the vertical, and that lean is largest at the bottom, with the legs folded. A 12 kg robot rising from a crouch of 0.12 m to 0.28 m needed up to 5.9 N m at the knee, against a peak of 20 N m. The heaviest body these knees could lift within their peak torque was 40.6 kg; a 65 kg robot could not rise at all, however slowly.

## Fix

Plan the rise for the real mass, payload included, and refuse it if the peak torque is over the motor's.

```cpp
const rc::legged::RisePlan p = rc::legged::rise_plan(leg, body_kg + payload_kg, knee, 0.12, 0.28, 1.0, 50);
if (!p.torque_ok || !p.heat_ok) fault();   // 5.9 N m for 12 kg, against a peak of 20
const double heaviest = rc::legged::max_rising_mass(leg, knee, 0.12, 0.28, 1.0, false);   // 40.6 kg
```

A shallower crouch needs less torque: a crouch of 0.20 m lifted a heavier body than one of 0.08 m.
