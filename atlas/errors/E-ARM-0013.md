## Symptom

When the target takes the arm near a straight elbow, the joints are told to move enormously and the arm jerks. The jerk comes when the elbow is nearly straight.

## Cause

The step was the pseudo-inverse, whose gain grows without bound near a singularity: 20.61 rad for an error of 1 cm.

The pseudo-inverse divides by the Jacobian's singular values. As one goes to zero, any error in its direction is multiplied by an enormous factor. Damping adds lambda squared to each, so the division never blows up, at the cost of accuracy in the weak direction.

## Fix

Damp the solve (lambda squared on the diagonal), limit each joint's step, and report when the result is poor in the hard directions. With damping the same error asked 0.021 rad.

```cpp
a[r][r] += lambda * lambda;   // inside the damped least squares solve
```
