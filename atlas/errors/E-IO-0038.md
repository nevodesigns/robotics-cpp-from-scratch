## Symptom

Sensor data that lines up when the program starts drifts out of line over minutes
and hours. Two devices that agreed at the start disagree later. A camera frame and
an IMU sample that should be simultaneous are a tenth of a second apart after an
hour, and a restart fixes it for a while.

## Cause

The offset between two clocks is not a constant. Every oscillator runs a little
fast or slow, a good crystal by tens of parts per million and a cheap oscillator by
thousands, so the offset grows steadily.

Measured, a device 50 ppm fast, stamping a 100 Hz stream for an hour through a link
that delays each message by two to ten milliseconds. The worst error in the send
time after the first ten minutes:

| using | worst error |
|---|---|
| when the message arrived | 8.0 ms |
| the first message's offset, for ever | 185.3 ms |
| the fitted map | 0.84 ms |

Using the arrival time is wrong by the jitter, which does not grow. Using the
offset once is wrong by `50e-6 x 3600 s = 180 ms` and by more the longer the
program runs. 185 ms is a whole video frame at five frames a second.

## Fix

Fit it. The arrival time is the send time plus a delay that is never negative, so
the best line through (device time, arrival time) lies under the data and touches
the least delayed samples. Keep the least delayed sample in each ten seconds of
device time and fit a line through the last ten minutes of those. The slope is the
drift, and it came out at 49.9 ppm for a device that was 50.0.

The fit still carries a constant error equal to the smallest delay, here 2 ms,
because nothing on one side of a link can know it without a second clock. For
fusing two sensors that share the host this cancels, and for absolute time it does
not, and the lesson says which it is.
