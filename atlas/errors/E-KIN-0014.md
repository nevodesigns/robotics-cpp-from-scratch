## Symptom

An arm that is crisp and well damped at the pose it was tuned in rings or crawls at
another. The gains were found carefully, on the bench, with the arm in its home
position, and they are wrong by the time the arm has moved. The faster the move the
more it shows.

## Cause

A joint's behaviour under proportional and derivative gains depends on the inertia
it is moving, and the inertia of an arm is not a constant. Damping, which sets how
much it overshoots, goes as `kd / (2 sqrt(kp * M))`, so a different `M` is a
different damping with the same gains.

Measured, a 0.5 m and 0.4 m arm with a 0.5 kg payload: the inertia the shoulder sees
is 1.176 kg m^2 with the elbow stretched out and 0.296 with it folded flat, **4.0
times** less, because folding the elbow brings the second link's mass in towards the
shoulder and a mass near the axis is easy to swing.

With gains of 60 and 12, a 0.3 rad shoulder step:

| elbow | M11 | overshoot | settled after |
|---|---|---|---|
| stretched | 1.176 | 1.9 % | 0.582 s |
| folded | 0.321 | none | 0.802 s |

Lightly damped where the arm is heavy and sluggish where it is light. Neither is a
mistake in the gains, which are the same.

## Fix

Either schedule the gains with the pose, using the mass matrix to compute the
inertia at the current elbow angle, or take the inertia out of the problem with the
inverse dynamics (`E-KIN-0015`), which makes the controller see a unit mass at every
pose and so be the same at all of them. Tuning at the pose with the greatest inertia
is safe from overshoot and slow elsewhere. Tuning at the lightest gives a ring at the
heaviest.
