## Symptom

A 10 ms timer is blocked for 100 ms by a slow handler, a file read or a dialog, and afterwards runs normally. A counter of ticks is nine short. Nothing logged, nothing delivered later to make up for it.

## Cause

A repeating `QTimer` does not queue the ticks it could not deliver. When the loop gets back to it there is one tick, and the next is a period after that. The ticks in the gap are gone.

Measured: a 10 ms ticker with one 100 ms block delivered **30** ticks and reported **10** missed, and the last slot was **40**. The ticks plus the missed slots equalled the last slot, so none were lost without being counted. Qt did not deliver a burst to catch up.

## Fix

Take the slot from the clock when a tick is delivered, and report the difference from the last one:

```cpp
const long long slot = slot_at(0, period_ns, clock.nsecsElapsed());
const long long skipped = slot - last_slot - 1;       // slots that went by without a tick
```

The consumer then decides what a skip means: stretch the step, hold the last value, or raise a fault.
