## Symptom

A heading estimate that is fine until the robot points roughly backwards, and then swings
through zero and out the other side for a moment, so the robot turns in a half circle in a
fraction of a second and snaps back. It happens at one heading, every time round, and it is
invisible if the robot never turns past it, which is why it is found in the field.

## Cause

A correction pulls the estimate towards the compass, and the pull is the compass minus the
estimate. Angles live on a circle, and 3.1 and -3.1 radians are 0.083 apart, across the back,
and not 6.2. The filter takes the 6.2 at its word and turns the whole way round to get there,
passing through zero on the way.

Measured, a robot turning at 0.2 rad/s for two minutes, nearly four turns, a gyro reading 0.02
rad/s high and a compass with 0.15 rad of noise, the error being the short way round:

| gain | plain difference rms | worst | short way rms | worst |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.005 | 1.264 | 2.174 | 0.609 | 0.759 |
| 0.020 | 0.518 | 1.346 | 0.189 | 0.230 |
| 0.100 | 0.709 | 3.139 | 0.048 | 0.162 |
| 0.500 | 0.455 | 3.081 | 0.091 | 0.302 |

At gains of 0.1 and above the worst error is 3.1 radians, half a turn, which is as wrong as a
heading can be: pointing straight away. And the plain difference with a small gain is no better
than the gyro alone, which was 1.461 rms and 2.416 at worst.

## Fix

Take the difference the short way, and keep the result in range:

```cpp
heading_ = wrap_pi(heading_ + gain_ * angle_difference(compass, heading_));
```

`wrap_pi` subtracts the whole number of turns at once, from the floor of `(angle + pi) / 2pi`,
which is exact for any angle and finishes in one step. Not a loop that takes off a turn at a
time, which never finishes for a big angle, and not `atan2(sin, cos)`, which loses digits as
the angle grows. The range is (-pi, pi], so that -pi and pi are one answer and not two.
