## Symptom

A numerically differentiated quantity that is wrong, or noisy, or both, and gets
worse when the obvious fix is applied. Making the step smaller makes the answer
worse, which is the opposite of what the word "step" suggests.

Downstream, a solver built on it converges slowly, wanders, or stalls.

## Cause

A derivative computed by differences has **two** errors that move in opposite
directions.

**Truncation.** The difference is not the derivative, and the gap shrinks as the
step shrinks. For a central difference it falls as the square of the step.

**Rounding.** The difference is between two nearly equal numbers, and the closer
they are the more of the significant digits cancel. That gets worse as the step
shrinks.

So there is a best step, and it is not the smallest one. Measured in lesson
13-03 against a derivative that can be written down exactly:

| step | error |
|---|---|
| 1e-1 | 1.21e-03 |
| 1e-2 | 1.21e-05 |
| 1e-4 | 1.21e-09 |
| **1e-6** | **3.39e-11** |
| 1e-8 | 4.32e-09 |
| 1e-10 | 6.26e-07 |
| 1e-12 | 1.33e-04 |
| 1e-14 | 4.17e-03 |

A step of 1e-14 is worse than a step of 1e-1. The best is in the middle, and for
a central difference on doubles it sits near 1e-6.

## Fix

Use a central difference and a step around 1e-6:

```cpp
nudged[joint] = held + step;
const Vec3 forward = f(nudged);
nudged[joint] = held - step;
const Vec3 backward = f(nudged);
nudged[joint] = held;

column = (forward - backward) / (2.0 * step);
```

The extra evaluation is worth it. A forward difference has error proportional to
the step rather than to its square, so at 1e-6 it is about six orders of
magnitude worse, and its best step is different and larger.

Two related things worth knowing.

**Put the value back.** Nudging a shared array and forgetting to restore it
gives a Jacobian whose later columns were computed at a configuration that no
longer matches the first.

**Scale the step to the variable.** 1e-6 is a reasonable absolute step for a
joint angle in radians, which is a quantity of order one. For a variable of
order a million, or a millionth, a relative step is what is meant, and the same
curve applies around it.
