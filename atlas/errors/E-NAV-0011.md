## Symptom

An obstacle appears in the map and never leaves. A van parks in the loading bay,
drives away, and the robot goes on routing around empty tarmac for the rest of
the shift. A door that was closed during mapping stays a wall.

Clearing it needs the map deleted and rebuilt. Driving through the space, with
the sensor plainly reporting it empty, does nothing.

## Cause

Two versions of the same mistake: evidence accumulates without limit, so
withdrawing it costs as much as building it.

**With probabilities multiplied together**, the limit is not merely large, it is
reached. Each observation is

```cpp
p = (p * l) / (p * l + (1 - p) * (1 - l));
```

and with a sensor right seven times in ten, forty four consistent observations
put `1 - p` below what a double can hold beside 1.0:

| observations | 1 - p | misses to recover |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 2.090e-04 | 10 |
| 40 | 1.887e-15 | 41 |
| 43 | 1.110e-16 | 44 |
| 44 | **0.000e+00** | **never** |
| 2000 | 0.000e+00 | never |

At forty four, `p` is exactly one. Every later update multiplies zero by
something and gets zero. The cell is not merely confident, it is unreachable,
and forty four observations is under two seconds of a 30 Hz scanner.

**With log odds and no clamp**, nothing overflows, and the arithmetic is still
symmetric: two hundred scans of a parked van need two hundred scans to undo. The
robot is routing around a memory for a minute.

## Fix

Keep log odds, and clamp them.

```cpp
cell += occupied ? kHit : kMiss;
if (cell > kClamp) cell = kClamp;
if (cell < -kClamp) cell = -kClamp;
```

The clamp is the whole defence. It bounds how sure a cell may become, so it
bounds how long it takes to change its mind. Measured with the clamp at plus and
minus four, a cell recovers in **five** contrary observations whether it was
confirmed ten times or two thousand, and a wall standing after two hundred scans
came down in six.

Choose the clamp from how quickly the world you work in changes, not from how
much you trust the sensor. A warehouse with pallets moving hourly wants a tight
clamp; a surveyed pipe rack can afford a loose one. The number is a statement
about the world, and it belongs next to a sentence saying so.

The related mistake is treating a cell nobody has looked at as free, which is
`E-NAV-0012`.
