## Symptom

The planner reports a route of 59 m and the robot drives 81. Estimated arrival
times are consistently optimistic, and by a varying amount: a route across open
ground is nearly right, one that zigzags around obstacles is badly out.

Two routes the planner calls equally good take visibly different times to drive.

## Cause

Every step onto a neighbouring cell is being charged one, including the four
diagonal ones, which are longer than the four straight ones by a factor of
`sqrt(2)`.

Measured on a room with two walls and a start and goal 55 cells apart:

| diagonal step | reported | true length | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 59.0000 | 81.3675 | **37.9%** |
| sqrt(2) | 73.0833 | 73.0833 | 0.0% |

The reported number understates the distance by more than a third. That alone
would be a labelling problem. The second effect is worse: since the search is
optimising the wrong quantity, **the route it picks is genuinely worse**, 11.3
percent longer than the shortest one actually available.

The cost function is what the search optimises. If it does not describe the
world, the answer is optimal for a world that does not exist.

## Fix

Charge geometry for geometry.

```cpp
const bool diagonal = dx != 0 && dy != 0;
return (diagonal ? std::sqrt(2.0) : 1.0) * multiplier;
```

Then make the cost and the length agree, and assert it in a test:

```cpp
RC_CHECK_NEAR(plan.cost, path_length(plan.cells), 1e-9);
```

Those are two different computations of the same quantity, one by the search and
one from the cells it returned, and they should agree to floating point. When
they stop agreeing, the cost function has picked up something that is not
distance, which may be deliberate: a terrain penalty, an unknown-space
multiplier, a preference for staying away from walls. All of those are
legitimate and all of them mean the cost is no longer metres, so stop reporting
it as metres.

Keep the two numbers separate in the API for that reason. `cost` is what the
search added up, `path_length` is what the robot will drive, and the caller
choosing between them has to know which question they are asking.
