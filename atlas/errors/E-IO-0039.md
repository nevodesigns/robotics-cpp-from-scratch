title: A device that restarted, and a timeline that did not notice
match: a restart the map is not told about is wrong by as long as the device was off
match: a device that restarts is noticed, and the timeline carries on from where it was
match: a counter can see a stall of just under half a turn, and no more
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 08-12-the-devices-own-clock

## Symptom

After a board browns out or is reset, every timestamp from it is wrong by a fixed
amount, seconds usually, and stays wrong for ten minutes or so before correcting
itself. Nothing is logged: the board is back, the messages are valid and in order.

## Cause

A restart is a step in the device's clock, and the fit was built from samples taken
before it. The unwrapped timeline carries on from where it was, and the seconds the
device was off are not in it, so every old sample is out by that long. The fit
averages them in with the new ones for as long as they stay in the window.

Measured, a device off for five seconds at thirty minutes and restarting its
counter. The worst error in the send time over the next ten minutes:

| | worst error |
|---|---|
| map kept, restart not noticed | 5011.0 ms |
| map reset on the restart | 3.5 ms |

The error is the downtime, to the millisecond.

The restart has to be noticed first. A serial link delivers in order, so a stamp
that went backwards is a reset and not a message that arrived late. And a counter
can only see a gap of just under half a turn: 32767 ticks of a sixteen bit
millisecond counter, 32.7 seconds. A silence longer than that looks like the device
starting again, and a restart looks like a silence.

## Fix

Have the unwrapper say when it saw one, and clear the map when it does:

```cpp
const Unwrapped u = unwrapper.accept(raw);
if (u.restarted) map.reset();
map.add(u.ticks / 1000.0, arrived);
```

The better fix is in the firmware: a boot counter in every message, so that a restart
is stated and not inferred.
