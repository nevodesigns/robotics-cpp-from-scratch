id: E-NAV-0005
title: An absolute fix fused as though it described the present
match: fusing a late fix as though it were current
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 16-03-the-measurement-that-arrives-late

## Symptom

Adding an absolute measurement to a dead reckoning estimate helps far less than
expected, or makes things worse. The estimate trails the robot by a distance
that grows with speed, and the faster the vehicle the further behind it sits.

Improving the measurement does not help. A better camera, a tighter scan match,
a more accurate beacon: the estimate still trails.

## Cause

The fix describes where the robot was when the picture was taken, and the fusion
treats it as where the robot is. Every correction pulls the estimate back into
the measurement's own past, and the prediction step spends the interval walking
it forward again.

Measured over forty seconds at 1 m/s, odometry at 500 Hz, a 10 cm fix at 10 Hz:

| fix age | fused as current | projected forward | rewound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.000 s | 0.0325 m | 0.0325 m | 0.0325 m |
| 0.040 s | 0.0445 m | 0.0339 m | 0.0338 m |
| 0.080 s | 0.0782 m | 0.0350 m | 0.0350 m |
| 0.150 s | 0.1449 m | 0.0369 m | 0.0425 m |
| 0.300 s | 0.2930 m | 0.0410 m | 0.0533 m |
| 0.600 s | 0.5920 m | 0.0494 m | 0.0691 m |

Dead reckoning alone managed 0.4478 m over the same run. **A fix 600 ms old,
fused as current, was worse than not fusing it at all.** How late is too late
depends on how fast the odometry drifts, so that crossing point moves; the
direction does not.

Eighty milliseconds is not a pessimistic figure for a vision fix. Exposure,
transfer, detection and the pose solve are each tens of milliseconds, and the
number is only ready at the end of all of them.

## Fix

Move the measurement to the present before fusing it, using the motion the
filter has already accumulated since the moment the measurement describes.

```cpp
const rc::nav::Estimate moved =
    rc::nav::project_forward(fix, motion_since_it_was_taken, motion_variance_since);
filter.correct(moved.value, moved.variance);
```

Keeping the motion history that requires is the price, and it is a small ring of
doubles as deep as the longest latency you tolerate.

Two details worth having right.

**Carry the variance forward too.** The motion you added was measured rather
than known, so the projected measurement is less certain than the original.
Inflating it does not make the estimate more accurate, and with poor odometry it
costs a little accuracy: measured, 0.1807 m against 0.1778 m. What it buys is
that the filter's statement about its own accuracy stops being a fiction.

**Rewinding the filter is not automatically better.** Restoring the state to the
measurement's moment, correcting there and replaying is exact only if you replay
everything that happened since, corrections included. A rewind that replays only
the motion discards every correction applied in the interval.

The rewound column above shows where that starts to matter. Up to 80 ms it
matches the projection exactly. From 150 ms it is worse and stays worse, and the
turn is at the fix interval: fixes arrive every 100 ms, so a rewind reaching
further back than that lands before a correction it is about to throw away.

The instrument that would have caught this is in `E-NAV-0007`, along with the
reason it usually does not.
