## Symptom

A report says the control loop's latency is 50 microseconds and the robot reacts visibly later than that. Or two designs, one that
obviously reacts slower, show the same figure.

## Cause

The time was taken when the reading was *looked at*, and not when it was *made*. Subtracting the time the previous reading was looked at
gives the loop's period. Subtracting the time of a stamp taken on arrival from the time of the work gives how long the work took.
Neither is how long the reading waited.

Latency is from the moment the sensor produced the value to the moment the controller had it, and the only way to know the first of
those is to stamp the reading **when it is made**, and carry the stamp with it:

```cpp
const Reading reading{now_ns()};         // in the producer, as it is made
...
latency.record(static_cast<double>(now_ns() - reading.stamp_ns) / 1000.0);   // in the consumer, as it is taken
```

Measured, 200 Hz for 2 seconds, the same producer and the same stamp, the consumer waiting three ways:

| consumer | p50 | p99 | max |
|---|---|---|---|
| spins | 0.4 us | 1.3 to 1.6 us | 12 to 50 us |
| polls every 1 ms | 514 to 549 us | 1080 to 1146 us | 1087 to 1254 us |
| sleeps on a condition | 39 to 65 us | 167 to 245 us | 473 to 1548 us |

A loop-time measurement would have read 5000 us for all three, the period.

## Fix

Stamp at the source. Carry the stamp in the data. Report a distribution, not a mean: lesson 07-03's histogram, with the 99th
percentile and the worst, and count the overflows so a long tail is reported rather than clipped.

If the stamp and the measurement are on different machines, the clocks have to be synchronised or the number is meaningless; that is
a later phase's problem, and the right response now is to say so.
