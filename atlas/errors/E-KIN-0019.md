## Symptom

An arm rated for a force of 20 N pushes a door open with ease in one place and cannot move it
in another, with the same motors, and nobody can say which of the two the rating was for. Or a
pushing task is planned from a single number and fails on the pose where the direction rotated.

## Cause

What a tool can push depends on the direction, and on the pose, and the dependence is the dual of
what it can do for speed. A direction that costs the motors a lot of torque per newton is a weak
one, and a direction that costs them nothing is the arm's own structure carrying the load.

Measured, motors of 20 and 8 N m, and joints held to 2 rad/s:

| elbow | push along | push across | move along | move across |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 720.1 N | 20.0 N | 0.02 m/s | 1.80 m/s |
| 0.50 | 72.8 N | 20.8 N | 0.22 m/s | 1.74 m/s |
| 1.00 | 37.6 N | 23.6 N | 0.43 m/s | 1.58 m/s |
| 1.57 | 25.6 N | 31.2 N | 0.62 m/s | 1.28 m/s |
| 2.50 | 20.0 N | 66.8 N | 0.80 m/s | 0.60 m/s |

Nearly straight, the arm can push along itself 36 times harder than across itself and can move
along itself 81 times slower. The direction it cannot move in is the direction it can push hardest.
Exactly straight, along the arm needs no torque at all, and the answer is no limit.

## Fix

Rate a strength in a direction, at a pose, and look for the weakest direction at the poses the
task uses, which for a straight arm is across it, at the full reach times the force. Use
`strongest_push` for the check, which scales a unit push until the first joint reaches its
limit, and plan pushing tasks so that the force is along the strong direction of the pose where
it is applied: a door pushed with the arm's weak direction is a door that will not move.
