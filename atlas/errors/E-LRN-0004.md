## Symptom

A network is trained, its test error is reported as a success, and nobody can say whether it is good. Later a simple controller turns out to do as well.

## Cause

There was no baseline. An error of 0.001 is good or bad only next to what the simplest learner gets on the same split. Least squares on the demonstrations costs one solve; within the expert's linear range it found the expert's gains, -2.000 and -3.000, to the third decimal.

A network that does no better than the line has spent its weights on nothing, and one that does a little better may not be worth its risks (38-07).

## Fix

Fit the line first, on the same split, and report the network's error beside it.

```cpp
const rc::learn::LinearPolicy base = rc::learn::fit_linear(train, 0.0);
report(rc::learn::linear_mse(base, test), network_test_error);
```

The line's weights are also readable: if they are far from what a controls engineer would choose, the data is the first thing to look at.
