title: An identifier that changed its last digit on the way in
match: an identifier beyond 2^53 loses its last digit as a double, and read_id keeps it

platforms: linux, windows

## Symptom

Two different devices with identifiers that differ in the last digit are treated as one, or a command for an identifier is acknowledged under another. Small identifiers work for ever.

## Cause

A `double` holds whole numbers exactly only up to 2^53, which is 9007199254740992. Beyond it, neighbouring whole numbers share a representation. A JSON reader that turns every number into a double hands back the nearest one that fits.

Measured: the identifier **9007199254740993** came back as **9007199254740992** through `toDouble`, and exactly through `toInteger`. A value of **3.7** and one of **12345678901234567890**, too big for 64 bits, were refused as not whole. A string "7" was refused as the wrong type.

## Fix

Read an identifier as an integer, and refuse anything that is not one:

```cpp
f.value = v.toInteger();
```

Better still, send large identifiers as strings.
