title: A low priority process that got almost nothing, because priority is a share

## Symptom

A background task, a logger, an uploader or a map builder, run at a low priority so as not to disturb the control loop, makes almost
no progress. Nothing is wrong with it. It is just never given the CPU, and the work it was supposed to finish in minutes takes hours.

## Cause

A lower priority is not "runs a little less". It is a **share**, and the shares are very unequal.

Measured on Linux, two CPU bound processes pinned to one core, 3 seconds, millions of loop iterations each:

| | first | second | the second's share |
|---|---|---|---|
| nice 0 against 0 | 748 | 751 | 50 percent |
| nice 0 against 10 | 1330 | 145 | **9.8 percent** |
| nice 0 against 19 | 1448 | 24 | **1.6 percent** |

The kernel gives each nice value a weight, 1024 at nice 0, 110 at nice 10, 15 at nice 19, and each process gets its weight over the sum
of the weights competing. 110 over 1134 is 9.7 percent and 15 over 1039 is 1.4, against the measured 9.8 and 1.6. The arithmetic
predicted the measurement.

That is with competition for a single core. On a machine with idle cores the low priority process runs happily and the problem never
shows, until the robot is busy.

## Fix

Choose the weight on purpose, with the table in front of you:

```cpp
rc::rt::cpu_share(0, 10);    // 0.903: the favoured process gets 90 percent
```

A background task that must finish needs a share it can finish on. Nice 10 gave it a tenth of a core, which may be enough, and nice
19 gave it a sixtieth, which usually is not. Or give it a core of its own, with CPU affinity, so that it competes with nothing.

Measure what the low priority work actually completed, as well as what the high priority work did. A system that looks fine on the
metric that was watched can be starving the one that was not.
