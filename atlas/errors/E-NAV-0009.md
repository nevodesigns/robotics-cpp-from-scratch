## Symptom

The planner produced a path that clears an obstacle. The robot follows it and
clips the obstacle. Nothing reports an error, the follower never left its
tolerance, and the recorded path looks correct.

The gap is worst at corners and disappears on straight sections.

## Cause

Pure pursuit aims at a point a lookahead ahead and drives an arc to it. Around a
corner, that arc is inside the corner. The robot is not failing to follow the
path; it is following the path the method defines, which is a smoothed version
of the one you gave it.

Measured on a right angle corner, steering by a perfect pose:

| lookahead | rms | worst | worst / lookahead |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 m | 0.0015 | 0.0176 | 0.176 |
| 0.40 m | 0.0119 | 0.1047 | 0.262 |
| 0.80 m | 0.0326 | 0.2125 | 0.266 |
| 1.50 m | 0.0828 | 0.4009 | 0.267 |
| 5.00 m | 0.4595 | 1.3536 | 0.271 |

**A right angle is cut by about 27 percent of the lookahead**, and the ratio is
stable across two decades of it. A 1 m lookahead means a quarter of a metre of
corner, every corner, by design.

The reason this is missed is that the follower's own error figure is not a
clearance figure. Cross track error is the distance to the nearest point on the
path, and the robot passing 25 cm inside a corner is 25 cm from the path in a
direction the planner cared about and the number does not describe.

## Fix

Decide what the number means before trusting it.

**Give the planner the follower's cut.** Inflate obstacles by the corner cut the
lookahead implies, about `0.27 * lookahead` for a right angle and less for a
gentler turn. That is a clearance the planner can reason about.

**Or shorten the lookahead where it matters.** Slowing for corners shortens it
too if the lookahead scales with speed, which is the same rule `E-NAV-0008`
arrives at from the opposite direction: slow down, look closer, cut less. This
is why speed profiles and path following are one problem rather than two.

**Measure clearance, not tracking.** A follower that reports 3 cm rms while
passing 20 cm inside a corner is reporting honestly about a question nobody
asked. If the requirement is about obstacles, the acceptance test has to measure
distance to obstacles.

And put a corner in the test path. A straight line, or a path made of gentle
curves, will not show any of this.
