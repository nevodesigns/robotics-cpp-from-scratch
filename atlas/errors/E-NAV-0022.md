## Symptom

A heading that is steady and slowly wrong, or one that is right on average and jittery. Raising
the gain cures the first and causes the second, and lowering it does the reverse, and each fix
looks like a bug fix until the other appears.

## Cause

The two sensors fail in opposite ways. A gyro is smooth and drifts: it integrates a turn rate,
so a bias of 0.02 rad/s becomes 2.4 radians in two minutes. A compass is noisy and does not
drift. The gain is how much of each reading is believed. Small trusts the gyro and lets the
drift in. Large trusts the compass and lets the noise in.

Measured, the short way round, rms error against the truth:

| gain | rms |
|---|---|
| gyro alone | 1.461 |
| 0.005 | 0.609 |
| 0.020 | 0.189 |
| 0.100 | 0.048 |
| 0.500 | 0.091 |

The error falls as the gain rises while the gyro's bias is the problem, bottoms out at 0.048, and
rises again when the compass's noise is. The best gain is thirty times better than the gyro alone,
and the gain at 0.5 is twice as bad as the best. It is an interior optimum, and which gain it is
depends on the two sensors' errors, which is what lesson 16-02's filter chose from.

## Fix

Choose it from the sensors: the gyro's bias and noise, the compass's noise, and the rate at which
each is read. Then measure it. The test sweeps four gains and shows the trade, which a robot with a
different gyro would find at a different place. A fixed gain tuned on one robot is a guess on the
next, and a filter that estimates the gyro's bias as it goes, which is the next step beyond this
lesson, removes the first half of the trade altogether.
