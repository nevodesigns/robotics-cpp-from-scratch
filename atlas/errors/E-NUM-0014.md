id: E-NUM-0014
title: A simulation that gains energy
match: explicit Euler does not merely have error, it adds energy
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 06-06-integration-and-the-energy-that-leaks
---

## Symptom

A simulated robot slowly shakes itself apart. A pendulum swings wider every
cycle, a suspension bounces higher, a compliant joint oscillates until the
numbers overflow. Nothing in the model is pushing it.

Reducing the time step makes it take longer to happen and does not stop it.

## Cause

Explicit Euler advances both halves of the state from where the step began:

```cpp
next.position = position + velocity * dt;
next.velocity = velocity + acceleration(position) * dt;   // acceleration at the OLD position
```

That is the obvious reading of the definition, and its error is not
symmetric. Every step it adds a little energy, and the addition compounds.

A mass on a spring at 2 rad/s, stepped at 0.01 s, energy as a fraction of where
it started:

| seconds | explicit Euler | semi-implicit | RK4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 54.5545 | 1.008696 | 1.000000 |
| 200 | 2976.1935 | 0.991266 | 1.000000 |
| 400 | 8857727.97 | 1.008327 | 1.000000 |
| 600 | **26362312744.26** | 1.001365 | 1.000000 |

Ten simulated minutes, and a spring nobody pushed is holding twenty six billion
times the energy it was given.

Halving the step halves the rate of the gain and does not remove it, so a
smaller step buys time rather than correctness. That is why the symptom looks
like "it is nearly stable, just needs tuning".

## Fix

Move one line.

```cpp
next.velocity = velocity + acceleration(position) * dt;
next.position = position + next.velocity * dt;            // the NEW velocity
```

Semi-implicit Euler, also called symplectic Euler. Same two multiplications,
same cost, same first-order accuracy, and the energy stays inside a small band
for ever: between 0.991 and 1.009 across the whole run above.

It never converges to the right energy and it never leaves, which is a different
and better guarantee than being accurate for a while.

Where accuracy matters as well as stability, `rk4_step` is fourth order and, at
equal work rather than equal step, five orders of magnitude better. But reach
for semi-implicit first: it is the method under most game physics and most robot
simulators, and this is why.

Two things worth checking afterwards.

**Watch a conserved quantity, not the trajectory.** Energy, or momentum, or
whatever your system should not be creating. A trajectory that looks plausible
for ten seconds tells you nothing; a conserved quantity that drifts tells you
immediately.

**Run it far longer than you plan to.** This defect is invisible over a second
and obvious over ten minutes, which is `E-NUM-0015`.
