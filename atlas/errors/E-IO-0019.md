title: One lost byte, two lost messages
match: a dropped byte loses nearly two frames
match: expected this to be true: .*lost_per_fault
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 08-06-break-your-own-link
---

## Symptom

A link that loses about one byte in a thousand loses about two messages in a
thousand, not one. The loss counter says so, and the figure never comes down to
what the byte count predicts. On the bench there is no loss at all, so nobody
looks.

## Cause

A frame announces its own length, and the parser believes it. When a byte
vanishes from the middle of a frame, the parser is still waiting for the bytes
it was promised, so it takes them from the **next frame**, starting with its
start byte. The first frame fails its checksum and the second has lost its
beginning. Both are gone.

Measured over ten thousand frames of ten bytes through the `Link` from lesson
08-04:

| damage | faults | lost frames | lost per fault |
|---|---|---|---|
| one byte in 500 dropped | 226 | 427 | 1.89 |
| one byte in 100 dropped | 1103 | 1900 | 1.72 |
| one bit in 100 flipped | 1113 | 1120 | 1.01 |

A flipped bit is cheaper because the frame keeps its length, so the checksum
rejects exactly that frame and the stream stays in step.

The figure is under two for a dropped byte because a drop in the start byte costs
only one frame, and at the higher rate two drops sometimes land in the same pair.

## Fix

Nothing in the checksum can fix this, because the checksum did its job. The
problem is what the parser does after a failure. Lesson 08-07 changes it to
rescan the bytes it consumed instead of throwing them away.

Until then, budget two frames for each byte the link can lose, and measure loss
with a port that actually loses bytes (`FaultPort`) rather than on a bench where
it never happens.
