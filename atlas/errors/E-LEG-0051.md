## Symptom

The robot is asked to go forward and sideways at once and it stumbles, or its feet land outside where the gait planned them, though each of the two speeds on its own is within its limit. Diagonal commands from a joystick are the usual trigger: the stick is pushed into a corner and both axes read full.

## Cause

The forward and sideways limits were applied separately, as a box. A leg can step further fore and aft than to the side, so the two speeds are not independent: what the leg must do is set by both together. With limits of 1.0 m/s forward and 0.4 m/s sideways, a command of 1.0 and 0.4 is inside the box and outside the ellipse `(vx / vx_max)^2 + (vy / vy_max)^2 <= 1`, by a factor of the square root of two. Passed on, it asks for a step that no leg can take.

Clipping each axis on its own is the other half of the mistake: it changes the direction of the command, so a diagonal turns into a slide.

## Fix

Scale the command back onto the ellipse, keeping its direction. The lesson's command of 1.0 and 0.4 became 0.71 and 0.28. Cap the turn rate on its own.

```cpp
rc::legged::CmdLimits lim;          // vx_max 1.0, vy_max 0.4
rc::legged::Cmd ask;
ask.vx = 1.0;
ask.vy = 0.4;
const rc::legged::Cmd use = rc::legged::limit_command(ask, now, lim, dt);   // 0.71 and 0.28, the same direction
```

The robot then goes more slowly on a diagonal than the stick suggests. That is the leg's limit and not a fault of the code.
