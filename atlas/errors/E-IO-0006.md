## Symptom

Your encoder and your decoder agree perfectly. The device rejects every frame,
or you reject every frame from the device. Nothing in either log says why,
because from each end's point of view the other is sending corrupt data.

In lesson 08-03 the test says:

```text
FAIL  the published check values, which is how you know you agree with anyone else
  expected static_cast<int>(crc8(view(bytes))) to equal 0xF4
    got:      116
    expected: 244
```

## Cause

A CRC is not one algorithm. It is a family, and four things pick a member:

- the **polynomial**, 0x07 or 0x31 or 0x1021 or dozens of others,
- the **starting value**, usually 0x00 or all ones,
- whether the bits are **reflected** on the way in and out,
- a value **xored at the end**.

Change any one and you have a checksum that is perfectly good, entirely self
consistent, and computed by nobody else on earth. Round tripping it against your
own decoder passes for every wrong variant, which is why this survives testing
and dies on contact with hardware.

Two mistakes account for most of it.

**The starting value.** CRC-16/CCITT is specified from 0xFFFF. Starting from
zero gives a working checksum with a different name.

**The direction.** In the unreflected form the byte is xored into the *top* of
the register and the register shifts left. The reflected form shifts right and
xors into the bottom, with the polynomial reversed. Mixing halves of the two is
the most common way to arrive here.

A third, specific to eight bit registers: shifting a `std::uint8_t` promotes it
to `int` first, so a register kept in an `int` keeps growing and the test of the
high bit stops meaning what it says.

```cpp
crc = static_cast<std::uint8_t>(crc << 1);   // the cast is doing work
```

## Fix

Check against the published check value, which every documented CRC has: the
result over the nine ASCII bytes `123456789`.

```text
CRC-8    polynomial 0x07,   init 0x00,   check 0xF4
CRC-16   polynomial 0x1021, init 0xFFFF, check 0x29B1
```

```cpp
RC_CHECK_EQ(static_cast<int>(crc8(digits)), 0xF4);
```

One assertion, and it distinguishes the correct implementation from every
self consistent wrong one. It is the only test here worth writing first.

When a datasheet gives a polynomial without a check value, the fastest way
forward is to ask the device for a frame and compute all four combinations of
starting value and reflection over it. One of them will match, and then the
check value over `123456789` is worth writing down for whoever comes next.
