title: One inverse kinematics branch solved, and a third of the workspace lost
match: the limits take a share of the workspace, and take it from the middle
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 13-05-the-angle-it-cannot-hold

## Symptom

The arm refuses targets. Not far ones, which would make sense, and not ones near
the base. Ordinary targets in the middle of its reach, scattered through the
workspace in a pattern that does not look like a pattern.

Moving the target a few centimetres often fixes it. Nobody can say why.

## Cause

The solver computes one of the two answers and returns it, and the limits reject
that one.

Lesson 13-02 established that a planar two link arm reaching a point almost
always has exactly two answers, elbow one way and elbow the other, and that the
maths does not prefer either. Joint limits do prefer one. They prefer a
different one in different parts of the workspace, and the boundary between
those regions runs through the middle of the reachable area rather than around
its edge.

Measured on a 1 cm grid, 0.5 m and 0.4 m links, shoulder [-0.6, 2.2] and elbow
[-2.4, 2.4]:

| | cells | share |
|---|---|---|
| reachable by geometry alone | 25140 | |
| legal within the joint limits | 13878 | 55.2% |
| both elbow branches legal | 5592 | 22.2% |
| elbow-up branch only | 4142 | 16.5% |
| elbow-down branch only | 4144 | 16.5% |

A solver that computes elbow-up and stops finds 9734 of the 13878 legal cells:
70.1 percent. Nearly a third of the arm's usable area is refused by code that
already had the answer and did not look at it.

The two "only" rows being almost equal is the reason this looks random. There is
no side of the workspace to avoid. The regions interleave.

## Fix

**Return both solutions and let the caller choose.** This is the same conclusion
as 13-02, arrived at from a different direction. There the reason was that
choosing between elbows is a decision about the world, which table to avoid,
which way the operator is standing. Here the reason is narrower and harder to
argue with: choosing early loses 30 percent of the machine.

```cpp
struct LegalSolutions {
  ArmSolution elbow_up;
  ArmSolution elbow_down;
  bool up_holds = false;
  bool down_holds = false;
};
```

**Keep unreachable and illegal apart.** A point beyond the links and a point the
limits refuse are different failures with different remedies: one needs a
different target, the other needs a different elbow. Collapsing them into a
single boolean is what makes this bug hard to see, because the message says
"cannot reach" about a point well inside the reach.

**Map the reachable set once and look at it.** The table above took a grid scan
and a few seconds. Doing it for a real arm, with its real limits, tells you
where the machine can and cannot work before anything is mounted around it, and
the answer is frequently not the disc everyone assumes.

**Then, and only then, worry about the nearest legal pose.** It matters for the
targets that are genuinely out of reach. It is the wrong tool for the ones that
were reachable by the other elbow all along.
