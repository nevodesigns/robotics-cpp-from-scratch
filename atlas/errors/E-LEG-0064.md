## Symptom

After a large push the body controller gives a weak or odd command and the robot recovers badly, though small pushes are handled well.

## Cause

The solver ran a fixed number of projected gradient iterations and its answer was used whether or not it had converged. For a horizon of 15 steps the lesson's problem needed 300 iterations; a harder problem, a larger push or a longer horizon, needs more, and the last iterate is then simply where the solver happened to stop.

## Fix

Check convergence: watch the size of the last step, start from the previous solution, and fall back to the fixed gain law when the solver has not converged in time.

```cpp
double u_all[20];
const double u = rc::legged::mpc_zmp(x, v, h, 0.05, 15, bound, 1.0, 1.0, 0.01, 300, u_all);
const double fallback = rc::legged::pd_zmp(x, v, 3.0, 0.9, bound[0]);
const double cmd = converged(u_all) ? u : fallback;
```

A real controller uses a proper quadratic programming solver with a warm start; the plain method here is for teaching, and its fixed iteration count is a number to check, not to trust.
