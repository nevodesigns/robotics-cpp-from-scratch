## Symptom

An arm moving smoothly along a path suddenly lurches, or a motor trips on
overspeed, or a controller saturates and the tool leaves the path entirely. It
happens at the same place every time, usually near full extension or with the
arm folded, and the commanded path through that point looks perfectly ordinary.

Slowing the whole motion down moves the lurch rather than removing it.

## Cause

At a singular pose the arm loses a direction: there is a way the tool cannot
move at all, however fast the joints turn. Approaching that pose, moving the
tool that way needs joint rates that grow without bound.

For a two link planar arm the determinant of the Jacobian works out to
`l1 * l2 * sin(q2)` exactly, so it depends on the elbow and nothing else, and it
is zero with the arm straight.

Measured on a 0.5 and 0.4 metre arm, tool moving outward at 0.1 m/s:

| reach | elbow | det J | q1 rate | q2 rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5000 | 1.9823 | 0.183303 | 0.09 | -0.27 |
| 0.8500 | 0.6741 | 0.124844 | 0.30 | -0.68 |
| 0.8990 | 0.0949 | 0.018947 | 2.11 | -4.74 |
| 0.8999 | 0.0300 | 0.005999 | **6.67** | **-15.00** |

A tenth of a millimetre from full extension, moving the tool a hundred
millimetres a second needs the elbow at about a hundred and forty revolutions a
minute. A tenth of that distance again needs ten times as much.

At full extension it is not large, it is impossible: the tool cannot move
outward at any joint speed. Sideways is still available, which is what "loses a
direction" means.

## Fix

Know where the singularities are and keep a measured distance from them.

```cpp
const double distance = rc::kin::manipulability(l1, l2, q2);
if (distance < floor) slow_down();
```

For this arm the measure is exact and free. For a general chain it is
`sqrt(det(J J'))`, computed from the Jacobian you already have.

**Refuse rather than divide.** A solver that inverts the Jacobian regardless
produces a number, and that number goes to the motors.

**Plan around them.** A path that passes near full extension can usually be
moved a few centimetres in, and the arm's usable workspace is smaller than its
reach for exactly this reason. Manufacturers quote both.

**Slow the tool near them**, which is what damping does automatically and is
`E-KIN-0007`.

**And test at the edge.** A path that stays in the middle of the workspace
exercises none of this. The test that finds it sweeps toward the limit and
watches the joint rate, which is four lines and one loop.
