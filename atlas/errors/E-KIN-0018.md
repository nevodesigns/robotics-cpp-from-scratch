## Symptom

A robot that estimates the force at its gripper from the motor currents does well over most of
its workspace and reports wild forces, or none, when the arm is nearly straight. A small
change in a motor current gives a big change in the estimated force, or an estimate that
bounces between large positive and negative values.

## Cause

Going from torques back to force inverts the transpose of the Jacobian, and its determinant is
`l1 * l2 * sin(q2)`, which is zero at full extension. There the force along the arm puts no torque
on either joint at all, because the load goes straight down the structure, so the torques contain
no information about it, and the inverse does not exist.

Near it, the inverse exists and is badly conditioned. Measured, with the elbow at 0.01 rad, a change of
0.1 N m in one torque changes the force it implies by **20.0 N**. A motor current has noise much
larger than that.

## Fix

Check the determinant before inverting, and say so when there is no answer: `force_for_torques`
returns a flag, and it is false at full extension. Do not hand a controller a force that is a
division by almost zero. Where a force must be known near a straight arm, measure it at the tool
with a force sensor there, which is a different instrument, or keep the arm away from the
straight pose. It is the same singularity as lesson 13-04's, seen from the other side.
