## Symptom

A straight move that includes some vertical travel takes much longer than the axes' speeds suggest. The vertical part of the move is short and the move is still slow.

## Cause

On a gantry each axis has its own speed and acceleration (belts on the long axes, a screw on z), and along a straight line every axis must keep its share of the direction. A move of 0.4 m in x and 0.4 m in z took 0.825 s with each axis on its own (by a curved path), and 0.900 s in a straight line, because z set the speed and x the acceleration.

Along a straight line the axes move in a fixed ratio, so each one must go at its share of the path speed. A slow axis (z on a screw) holds the line back by its speed, and a weakly accelerated one (x on a belt) by its acceleration; one axis can set each limit, and the line takes longer than any axis alone.

## Fix

Work out a line's time from every axis's share of the direction, and keep vertical parts of moves short or make them separate when the path in between does not matter.

```cpp
const double d[3] = {0.4, 0.0, 0.4};
const double t = rc::arm::straight_line_time(machine, d);   // 0.900 s
```

When one axis sets both limits, or the axes are equal, a straight line costs nothing extra.
