id: E-IO-0010
title: A watchdog fed by bytes instead of by messages
match: noise that never becomes a frame leaves the link stale
match: expected .*last_message_at.*
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 08-04-the-link-that-goes-quiet
---

## Symptom

A robot keeps running on stale data. The link's health check says everything is
fine, and it says so most confidently in exactly the situations where things are
worst: while a motor is running, or after the controller board has crashed.

Nothing in the log looks wrong. The last recorded pose is minutes old and the
watchdog never fired.

## Cause

The staleness clock was fed by bytes:

```cpp
if (port.read(buffer, size) > 0) last_seen_ = now;   // the bug
```

Bytes are not information. Three ordinary situations produce a steady stream of
them and no messages at all:

- A crashed board holding its line at a level the receiver reads as a
  continuous run of identical bytes.
- Interference from a motor or a switching supply on an unshielded cable.
- A baud rate mismatch, which turns every real message into a different set of
  bytes that happen to arrive at the right times.

In all three the watchdog is fed continuously, so it reports a healthy link
while the controller acts on the last pose it ever received. The noisier the
fault, the healthier the link appears, which is the opposite of what a safety
check is for.

## Fix

Feed the clock from the last thing that was actually a message: framed
correctly, checksum intact, decoded.

```cpp
if (!parser_.push(byte)) continue;

rc::span<const std::uint8_t> body;
if (!verify_and_strip(parser_.payload(), body)) { ++checksum_failures_; continue; }

++frames_;
last_message_ = now;      // here, and nowhere else
```

Nothing else counts, because nothing else is evidence that somebody is at the
other end.

Then keep the other numbers separately, because their ratios are a diagnosis
rather than a status:

| what you see | what it means |
|---|---|
| discards high, frames zero | wrong baud rate, or nothing connected |
| frames arriving, checksums failing | physical: a cable, a ground, a motor |
| frames and checksums fine, nothing recent | the device is alive and has stopped sending |
| everything zero | not polling, or the port never opened |

A link that reports only "ok" or "failed" cannot distinguish any of those, and
the distinction is the difference between an afternoon and a week.

One related mistake worth avoiding at the same time: a link that has never been
polled is not stale. Starting the clock at zero and comparing against a real
timestamp makes every link born dead, and the robot refuses to start.
