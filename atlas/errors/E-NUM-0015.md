## Symptom

An integrator was chosen by measuring its error against a known answer over a
short run. It won. In the long simulation it is the one that diverges.

Or the reverse: a method is dismissed for being less accurate in the benchmark
and it was the only stable one on offer.

## Cause

Accuracy and stability are different properties, and a short test measures only
the first.

Error in position after one second, at a step of 0.02:

| method | error |
|---|---|
| explicit Euler | 1.596e-02 |
| semi-implicit Euler | 1.831e-02 |

Explicit Euler wins. Over ten minutes it has gained twenty six billion times its
energy and the other has not moved, which is `E-NUM-0014`.

The order of a method says how its error shrinks with the step and says nothing
about what it does to a conserved quantity. Both of those Euler methods are
first order:

| dt | Euler | ratio | semi-implicit | ratio | RK4 | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.020 | 1.596e-02 | | 1.831e-02 | | 3.937e-08 | |
| 0.010 | 8.158e-03 | 1.96 | 9.124e-03 | 2.01 | 2.443e-09 | 16.11 |
| 0.005 | 4.121e-03 | 1.98 | 4.554e-03 | 2.00 | 1.521e-10 | 16.06 |
| 0.0025 | 2.071e-03 | 1.99 | 2.275e-03 | 2.00 | 9.490e-12 | 16.03 |
| 0.00125 | 1.038e-03 | 2.00 | 1.137e-03 | 2.00 | 5.932e-13 | 16.00 |

Half the step, half the error, for both. Half the step, a sixteenth of the
error, for the fourth-order method. None of those numbers distinguishes the
method that conserves energy from the one that manufactures it.

## Fix

**Measure over the horizon you will run.** If the robot simulates for an hour,
the acceptance run is an hour, not the ten seconds that fit in a unit test.

**Watch a conserved quantity as well as the error.** Energy, momentum, the norm
of a quaternion, the determinant of a rotation. A quantity that should not
change is a far better instrument than a trajectory that should.

**Compare at equal work, not at equal step.** RK4 evaluates the acceleration
four times per step, so a fair comparison gives Euler four times the steps. Done
that way, RK4 at 0.02 s is 3.937e-08 against Euler at 0.005 s at 4.121e-03: five
orders of magnitude for the same four calls. Comparing at equal step makes a
cheap method look competitive when it is not.

**And say which property you chose for.** "We use semi-implicit Euler because it
is stable over long runs, not because it is accurate; measured, it is slightly
less accurate than explicit Euler over one second" is a comment that survives the
next person's benchmark.
