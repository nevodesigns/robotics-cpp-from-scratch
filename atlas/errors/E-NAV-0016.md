title: A route through ground nothing has looked at

match: no route at all is an answer, not a crash

## Symptom

The planner returns a shorter route than the one an operator would have picked,
and the robot takes it straight into a part of the building no sensor has ever
seen. Round a blind corner, off the edge of a dock, through a doorway that is
glass.

The route is genuinely the shortest one on the map. That is the problem.

## Cause

Unknown cells are being costed as free, so unexplored space is not merely
allowed, it is **preferred**: it has no obstacles in it, because nothing has
looked.

Measured on a corridor surveyed only along the bottom, with an obstacle across
the surveyed part and unseen ground above it:

| unknown is | length | cells crossed that nothing has seen |
|---|---|---|
| blocked | 57.49 | 0 |
| expensive, x4 | 57.49 | 0 |
| expensive, x1.05 | 55.00 | 25 |
| free | 55.00 | 25 |

The shortcut is shorter precisely because it is unmapped.

A grid of booleans produces this by construction: unknown and free are the same
value, and there is nothing to decide. That is `E-NAV-0012` seen from the
planner's side.

## Fix

Make it a decision with a name, and set it per caller.

```cpp
PlanOptions options;
options.unknown = UnknownIs::blocked;
```

- **blocked** for anything that carries a load, moves near people, or cannot be
  recovered by hand. It is the right default and it is never surprising.
- **expensive** where exploring is part of the job. Then the multiplier is where
  the real decision lives, and it is sharper than it looks: on the map above
  every multiplier at 1.10 and over took the surveyed route and everything below
  it took the shortcut. Sweep it on your own map rather than guessing.
- **free** essentially nowhere.

Two things that go with it.

**A refusal is an answer.** When no route exists the planner must say so and the
caller must handle it, rather than the failure arriving as an empty path that
something else treats as "already there". Check `found`, and give the caller
`expanded` too so "no route" can be told apart from "gave up early".

**Refuse a start or goal outside the map** rather than indexing with it. That is
the same bounds discipline as `E-NAV-0013`, and a planner is exactly the place
where an out-of-range goal arrives from somewhere else.
