title: A controller that holds the arm a third of a metre below where it was told
match: a proportional controller sags under gravity, and feeding the holding torque forward removes it
match: expected this to be true: sag_out

## Symptom

The arm is commanded to a pose and settles somewhere else, steadily, a long way
below it. The controller reports no fault. The joint errors are not zero and not
growing, the motors are warm, and raising the gain shrinks the miss without ever
closing it. It is worse stretched out and gone when the arm points straight up.

## Cause

A proportional controller supplies torque only in proportion to an error, so to
hold an arm against gravity it must have an error. The torque gravity asks for is
the gain times the sag, and the arm sags until those are equal: the sag is the
torque over the gain.

Measured, a 0.5 m and 0.4 m arm with a 0.5 kg payload and a gain of 50 N m per
radian on each joint, ten simulated seconds to settle:

| pose | shoulder torque needed | proportional only | torque fed forward |
|---|---|---|---|
| straight out | 17.56 N m | 326.6 mm | 0.0000 mm |
| raised 45 degrees | 12.42 N m | 314.7 mm | 0.0000 mm |
| elbow bent | 6.31 N m | 89.8 mm | 0.0000 mm |
| straight up | 0.00 N m | 0.0 mm | 0.0000 mm |

The miss follows the torque, so it is largest where the arm is stretched out. A
test that only checks the arm at its upright home pose sees none of it.

## Fix

Supply the torque gravity needs, and let the feedback correct only what is left.
The holding torque is a function of the pose, so it is computed from the pose the
arm is being told to take and added to the controller's output, which is a
feedforward with a physical meaning:

```cpp
const JointTorques hold = holding_torques(arm, target.q1, target.q2, payload);
shoulder_command = kp * error1 - kd * rate1 + hold.shoulder;
```

An integral term also removes a steady sag, and does it slowly and with
windup, after the arm has first dropped. Feedforward removes it
before it happens.
