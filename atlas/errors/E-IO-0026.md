id: E-IO-0026
title: A counter too narrow to see how much was lost
match: an eight bit counter reports a fraction of a long stall
match: a counter can only see a gap shorter than half its range
match: expected .*widest_gap.*
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 08-08-a-device-faster-than-you-read
---

## Symptom

The loss counter says the link lost a few dozen messages a minute and the
robot's behaviour says it lost far more. After a long stall the figure is small,
or zero, for a stall that plainly cost seconds of data.

## Cause

A counter of N bits can tell a gap from a message going backwards only while the
gap is shorter than half its range. Past that the arithmetic cannot say which
way the message went.

| counter | widest gap it can see | a gap of exactly its range looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 8 bits | 127 | nothing at all |
| 16 bits | 32767 | nothing at all |

Measured, a reader that came round every two seconds lost 6290 frames in twenty
seconds, ten stalls of about 629. A sixteen bit counter said 6290. An eight bit
counter said **1170**, which is 629 modulo 256 per stall, 117, and would have said
nothing for a stall whose remainder was 128 or more.

The danger is that the counter is not wrong in an obvious way. It reports a
plausible small number, from the one device whose stalls are the long ones.

## Fix

Choose the width from the longest stall you must account for, not the cheapest
field:

    widest gap needed = frames per second x longest stall in seconds

At 500 frames a second a one second stall is 500 frames, which an eight bit counter
cannot see and a sixteen bit one sees with room to spare. Then keep the
`widest_gap()` of the counter you chose in the same place as the stall budget it
protects, so that changing one prompts a look at the other.
