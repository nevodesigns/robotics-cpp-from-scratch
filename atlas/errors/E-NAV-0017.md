id: E-NAV-0017
title: A route planned for a robot with no width
match: a robot with a width does not fit through a planner's shortest path
match: inflation grows obstacles by a disc, not a square
platforms: linux, windows
teaches: 16-07-the-rover-that-fits
---

## Symptom

The planner returns a route through a gap the robot does not fit through, and
the robot wedges itself in a doorway or scrapes down the side of a rack. The
route is correct: every cell on it is free.

Widening the gap in the map by hand fixes that doorway and not the next one.

## Cause

A search over grid cells plans for a point, because a cell is a point. The robot
is a disc, and nothing in the search knows that.

Measured on a room with a 0.40 m gap straight ahead and a 1.50 m gap off to one
side, start and goal 6.9 m apart:

| robot radius | route | length | clearance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 m | found | 6.900 m | **0.200 m** |
| 0.15 m | found | 7.977 m | 0.224 m |
| 0.25 m | found | 8.060 m | 0.316 m |
| 0.35 m | found | 8.143 m | 0.412 m |
| 0.50 m | **none** | | |

The shortest route is shortest **because** it goes through the gap the robot
does not fit through. That is not bad luck; a narrow gap is usually the direct
way, which is why it exists.

## Fix

Grow the obstacles by the robot's radius before planning, and search the grown
map.

```cpp
const auto planning = rc::nav::inflated(map, robot_radius);
const auto plan = rc::nav::plan_path(planning, start, goal, options);
```

That moves the geometry into the map once instead of into every step of the
search for ever, and it makes a robot too wide for any route get **no route**
rather than a bad one, which is the answer you want.

Four details in the inflation, each a decision rather than an implementation
note.

**A disc, not a square.** Skipping the `dx*dx + dy*dy > radius*radius` test
over-blocks the diagonals by a factor of root two and closes gaps the robot
would have fitted through.

**Round the radius up to whole cells.** Half a cell does not exist, and rounding
down produces a map that says the robot fits when it does not. Inflation is then
conservative by up to one cell, which belongs in the margin rather than being a
surprise.

**Off the edge of the map is occupied.** A robot half over the border is
somewhere the map cannot describe.

**Unknown grows as unknown, not as occupied.** A cell beside unseen ground is
not known to be blocked, and saying it is makes the map more certain than the
evidence. What the planner then does with unknown is `E-NAV-0016`.

And do not stop there: the inflation covers the width and nothing else. What
else spends the same margin is `E-NAV-0018`.
