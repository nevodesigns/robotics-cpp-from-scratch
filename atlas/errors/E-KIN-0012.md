## Symptom

The arm lifts its rated payload in the demonstration and stalls with it in the
field, or trips a current limit when the operator moves it out to full reach. The
same load is fine folded in and impossible stretched out, and a bigger payload
fails at a different place each time, so nobody can say what the rating means.

## Cause

A payload rating is a single number, and the torque it demands is not. The torque
is a straight line in the payload, `alone + per_kg * p`, with different
coefficients at every pose, so each pose has its own heaviest payload.

Measured, motors of 20 N m at the shoulder and 8 N m at the elbow on the same arm:

| pose | heaviest payload |
|---|---|
| straight out | 0.776 kg |
| raised 45 degrees | 1.715 kg |
| elbow bent | 2.283 kg |
| straight up | no limit |

A rating of 2.2 kg is true of the bent elbow and nearly three times too high for
the pose with the longest lever. The limit is a property of the pose and the
motors together, and a number quoted without a pose is the best case.

## Fix

Say which pose, and since the torque is linear, solve for the payload and do not
search for it:

```cpp
// torque = alone + per_kg * p, per joint
return per_kg > 0.0 ? (limit - alone) / per_kg : (-limit - alone) / per_kg;
```

Take the smaller joint. Return zero when the arm cannot hold itself, and infinity
when neither joint feels the payload. That last case is `cos(pi / 2)`, which is
6e-17 and not zero, so compare with a tolerance, or a pose that should be
unlimited reports a payload in the millions. Then rate the arm at its worst
reachable pose, which is stretched out, and not at the one in the photograph.
